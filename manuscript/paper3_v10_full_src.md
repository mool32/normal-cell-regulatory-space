# Human malignancies show increased occupancy of a shared region of normal-cell regulatory space

**Short title (running head):** Malignancy raises occupancy of a normal cell state

**Significance Statement.** Using 44 regulatory modules scored in single-cell RNA data, we built a map of normal human cell states from normal cells alone — no cancer enters its construction — and projected individual cancer cells onto it. Within patients, malignant epithelium occupies one particular region of that map far more often than the same patient's own matched normal epithelium: in colorectal cancer a median 19% against 5%, higher in 43 of 46 patients, and the difference survives removing overall transcriptional output. The same region is occupied by cancers of twenty-five lineages. Which normal cell lies nearest it is less certain: under our catalog it is the placental cytotrophoblast, whose physiology natively accommodates invasion and proliferation, but independently reconstructed catalogs reproduce the region while naming a different cell inside it. Malignancy therefore raises occupancy of a pre-existing normal physiological state rather than creating a cancer-specific one. Whether that state is specifically trophoblast-like, and whether cancers thereby reactivate an embryonic program, remain open questions we treat as such.

## Abstract

We compared cellular regulatory states using 44 transcriptional modules representing major signaling and stress-response pathways. Their activities across 154 normal human cell types, from single-cell RNA-sequencing data alone, were decomposed by principal component analysis into a nine-dimensional reference space constructed without reference to any cancer. In matched samples, malignant epithelium occupied one region of this space far more often than the patient's own normal epithelium: in colorectal cancer a median 19% against 5%, higher in 43 of 46 patients (paired *P* = 9 × 10⁻¹³), +10.5 points with transcriptional magnitude removed (44 of 46), holding against the crypt-base progenitors these tumors arise from, and reproduced in every tissue tested except prostate; pooled across four paired datasets a mixed-effects model credits the dataset clustering and estimates +13.0 percentage points (*P* = 1.9 × 10⁻¹²). Malignant cells from 25 cancer datasets spanning diverse lineages occupy the same region, at a mean pairwise distance of 0.16 against 0.99 among their normal tissues of origin — but most of that contraction is shared transcriptional output and matched averaging rather than lineage-independent convergence, and the magnitude-removed residue is smaller and sensitive to specification. Ranking normal cell types by cross-cancer recurrence and depth put the placental cytotrophoblast nearest the region, while independent reconstructions of the catalog from the same criteria reproduce the region and name a different cell in it, so the region is robust and the identity of the nearest cell is conditional on this catalog. Malignancy thus raises occupancy of a pre-existing normal physiological state that accommodates key malignant phenotypes, rather than producing a wholly cancer-specific identity.

## Introduction

A mammalian organism comprises cells occupying numerous epigenetically determined differentiation states, each characterized by a distinct transcriptional phenotype. Cancers arising in different tissues retain many epigenetic and phenotypic features of their cells and tissues of origin. At the same time, malignancy imposes a set of broadly shared functional requirements, including sustained proliferation, invasion and motility, resistance to hypoxic and proteotoxic stresses, immune evasion, and metabolic reprogramming (Hanahan & Weinberg, 2000, 2011; Hanahan, 2022). None of these properties is intrinsically cancer-specific: each is physiologically deployed by particular normal cell types under particular conditions. This raises a fundamental question: to what extent is the state of a malignant cell determined by its tissue-specific ancestry, and to what extent does malignant transformation drive cells of different origins toward a common epigenetic and functional state?

Single-cell transcriptomics provides a direct readout of the transcriptional manifestations of cellular epigenetic states and, in principle, makes this question experimentally accessible. Direct comparison of whole transcriptomes, however, is poorly suited to identifying convergence at the level of regulatory state. Transcriptomes are dominated by lineage-specific gene expression, technical variation, and differences in transcriptional complexity, whereas functionally related states may arise through coordinated changes in multiple regulatory pathways without requiring global transcriptomic similarity. A biologically interpretable comparison therefore requires reducing transcriptomes from thousands of individual genes to a common set of functional regulatory programs whose activities can be compared across otherwise unrelated cell types.

To accomplish this, we developed a program-based coordinate system of cellular state. Rather than allowing cancer datasets themselves to define the dimensions of comparison, we first constructed a cancer-independent reference from normal human cells. We selected 44 transcriptional modules representing major cellular signaling and stress-response mechanisms, each defined mechanistically around a regulatory circuit comprising a signal-sensing component, transcription-factor output and targets, cyclical negative feedback, and receptor-field specificity. Module activities were inferred from single-cell RNA-sequencing data for 154 normal human cell types and used to construct a low-dimensional eigenspace representing the major axes along which normal cellular regulatory states vary. Once established, this reference coordinate system remains fixed: individual cells from independent datasets can be projected into it without retraining, permitting their regulatory states to be compared with one another and with the spectrum of normal human cell states.

This strategy differs conceptually from conventional transcriptome clustering or dimensional reduction performed jointly on normal and malignant cells. Cancer contributes neither to selection of the regulatory dimensions nor to construction of the reference geometry. Consequently, any recurrent localization of independently generated cancers within that geometry constitutes an out-of-sample observation rather than a structure imposed by cancer-derived variation. It also makes the analysis directly falsifiable: preservation of tissue identity should leave cancers dispersed near their respective normal origins, whereas acquisition of a common regulatory state should cause independently arising malignancies to converge within the fixed normal-cell space. Prior work has projected tumors onto normal references organ by organ (Young et al., 2018; Kildisiute et al., 2021) and has catalogued recurrent malignant programs derived from cancer data themselves (Kinker et al., 2020; Barkley et al., 2022; Gavish et al., 2023); an influential alternative view holds that malignant states remain lineage-local, near each tumor's own cell of origin (Patel & Yanai, 2024). We differ in resolving single malignant cells of twenty-five lineages onto a mechanistically interpretable, cancer-blind frame, and in locating that convergence in a specific, differentiated, physiological region of normal state rather than at a pluripotent abstraction (Chen & He, 2016).

Applying this approach to single-cell transcriptomes from 25 cancers of diverse origins, and to paired tumour and normal epithelium from the same patients, separates three claims that are easily conflated and that we keep apart throughout: that malignant cells occupy one shared region of normal regulatory state more often than their matched normal counterparts, which the data support directly; that the normal cell lying nearest that region is the placental cytotrophoblast, which holds under our module catalog but not under independently reconstructed ones; and that cancers thereby reactivate an embryonic or trophoblast-like program, which remains a hypothesis and is treated as one. The convergent state is therefore not a cancer-created transcriptional state but a pre-existing physiological state represented among normal human cells, and the regulatory features that distinguish it within the normal-cell reference are reproduced during cancer convergence. These observations provide a quantitative framework in which the long-standing concept that malignancy involves reacquisition and modification of embryonic cellular programs (Abelev, 1971; Sharma & Ginhoux, 2022) becomes testable; we return to it in the Discussion as an interpretation, not as a result.

## Results

Three claims of different strength run through what follows, and we keep them apart throughout. The **first** — that malignant cells occupy one shared region of normal regulatory state far more often than their own matched normal counterparts — is settled in §§2 and 5 by direct measurement, and is the claim this paper defends. The **second** — that the normal cell nearest that region is the placental cytotrophoblast — is established in §3 *under this module catalog*, and independently reconstructed catalogs reproduce the region while naming a different cell inside it. The **third** — that cancers thereby reactivate an embryonic or trophoblast-like program — is not a result but an interpretation, and it appears only in the Discussion.

### A cancer-independent program space recovers normal cell-state biology

We define a cell's state by 44 regulatory modules. A module is a self-contained regulatory unit specified by four operational criteria: a signal-sensing component; a transcription-factor output with documented target genes; a cyclical negative-feedback element; and receptor-field specificity. Approximately 75 candidate signaling and metabolic axes were screened against these criteria (KEGG, Reactome and the primary literature). A candidate was retained when it represented a biologically distinct regulatory input or response; when the direction of its response could be read from a defined transcriptional output rather than from the expression of a single pathway component; and when its sensor, downstream transcriptional regulator and target-gene response were characterized well enough to fix that direction. Candidates lacking a defined transcriptional output, substantially duplicating a module already retained, or acting primarily post-transcriptionally were dropped. This yielded 44 modules (Tables S9, S9b), fixed before any cancer data were examined and chosen for interpretability and transcriptional observability — not for the ability to discriminate cancers or particular normal cell types. The panel spans growth-factor and developmental signaling, inflammatory and innate-immune signaling, cell-cycle control, nutrient and energy sensing, lipid and sterol metabolism, hypoxic and oxidative stress, proteotoxic stress and the unfolded-protein responses, DNA-integrity responses, nuclear-receptor endocrine signaling, and autophagic and other homeostatic programs.

Three properties of the panel itself can be checked on normal cells alone. First, the selection produces genuine *units*: across the 154 normal cell types the genes of a module co-vary far more than size-matched random gene sets (median within-module coherence +0.154 versus +0.014; *P* = 3 × 10⁻¹⁵) and more than the modules of any established pathway collection scored identically — KEGG +0.062, Reactome +0.058, Hallmark +0.070, PROGENy +0.051 — despite carrying twenty- to thirty-fold fewer genes each (SI). Second, the transcriptional-output requirement is what makes a module *readable*: the induced arm of a module — its feedback element and transcription-factor targets — varies across normal cell types substantially more than its structural arm of sensors and cascade components (median SD 0.287 versus 0.222; *P* = 3 × 10⁻⁵), which is why pathways read out through induced targets rather than through the abundance of their receptors or kinases. That comparison is over the genes the catalog *annotates* (387 induced against 704 structural), not over the slots it actually scores: 104 of the 216 scored slots are induced genes and six modules carry none (§4), so the argument supports the design of the catalog rather than the coverage of the instrument built from it. Third, the catalog's size is adequate rather than arbitrary: random subsets of the 44 recover the normal-cell geometry progressively and reach 95% of the full panel's biological-family recovery, and 95% of its cell-to-cell geometry, only at 35 modules, with both still improving at 44 (SI).

We state the limits of the selection as plainly as its basis. The negative-feedback criterion was the organizing idea of the panel — motivated by the recurrent observation that induced negative regulators behave as brakes whose loss releases the pathway (Amit et al., 2007; Lito et al., 2013; Chandarlapaty, 2012) — rather than an independently validated selector. A canonical feedback element can be named for 38 of the 44 modules and not for six; each is listed, with its gene and its status, in Table S9b, and two attempts to validate the criterion independently are reported in Note S-N. Nor is an individual module a classifier of cell identity: one five-gene module separates biological families no better than five random expressed genes (η² 0.33 versus 0.33). What the panel supplies is a set of coherent, individually interpretable axes; the biology emerges from the space they span, not from any module alone. Scoring, the 154 × 44 matrix and its decomposition into the nine-dimensional reference space are specified in Methods (Fig. 1A). The frame differs from label-transfer annotation (Aran et al., 2019) and from open-loop pathway or regulon scoring (Schubert et al., 2018; Aibar et al., 2017; Lotfollahi et al., 2023) in being a fixed, linear, biology-defined frame built once from normal cells and never refit.

Before any disease data are introduced, the map is biologically meaningful, in three independent ways (Fig. 1B). Its **axes are individually interpretable**: the first component (29% of variance) runs from inflammatory cells to terminal germline cells, the second (14%) from quiescent to proliferative cells, the third from signaling-dominated immune cells to metabolic epithelia — and an external quality signature scored without reference to the modules orders each axis monotonically (immune signalling on PC1, Spearman ρ = −0.89; translational output on PC2, +0.75). An independent biological-family label explains a large fraction of where cells fall on every axis (η² = 0.31–0.70, each against its own label-permutation null and each above all 2,000 draws of it — *P* < 5 × 10⁻⁴, the floor 2,000 draws can reach; Figs. S1-II and S3). The modules are also not restating one another: of the 946 module pairs only six exceed |*r*| > 0.7 across the normal cell types, and two of those share a gene by construction (Fig. S2). Its **global layout is biological**: families form coherent territories, and a cell's nearest neighbour in the full space shares its biological family 62% of the time (95% CI 55–70%) versus 14% under a family-size-preserving label permutation (*P* = 1 × 10⁻⁴, the floor of 10,000 draws). This last statistic shows that the space is organized, but it does not distinguish *this* panel from others: size- and expression-matched random gene panels put through the identical pipeline reach ~61%, and a generic highly-variable-gene decomposition reaches 68–71% (SI). The evidence that the panel is the right frame is its interpretability and coherence, not this concordance figure. Constructed from normal cells and not one cancer cell, the map already recovers structure it was never told — the fact on which everything below rests.

### Diverse cancers converge onto one region of normal-cell state space

We then project into this frame the malignant single cells of twenty-five quality-controlled cancers (Fig. 2). They span essentially every lineage — epithelial, hematopoietic, glial, neural-crest, two independent mesenchymal sarcomas, two independent germ-cell tumors, and neuroendocrine — and their normal cells of origin are, accordingly, near-orthogonal in the eigenspace (mean pairwise cosine distance 0.99; cosine distance runs from 0 to 2, so 0.99 is the value expected for orthogonal states, and three of the 300 origin pairs share an assigned origin by construction and contribute exactly 0). Their malignant compartments are not: they cluster to a mean pairwise distance of ~0.16, against ~0.99 among their near-orthogonal origins. This raw contraction is largely a matched-averaging and shared-magnitude effect, and we are explicit about it: twenty-five equal-sized aggregates of *unrelated* normal cells, formed by the same averaging, cluster nearly as tightly (a matched-averaging null of coherent normal micro-populations reaches ~0.13), and the malignant clustering does not tighten as more cells are averaged — it is flat from 25 to 4,000 cells per cancer (SI), so it is a stable biological quantity, not the shrinkage of large means. What is *not* an averaging artifact is the module **shape**: with the overall-magnitude component removed, the malignant states cluster more tightly than the same matched-averaging null — a real, if modest, convergence in regulatory *pattern* beyond shared output (Fig. 2). Its size depends on how magnitude-removed scores are rescaled; we report all three specifications we ran rather than the most favourable, and two of the three — including the simplest, unrescaled one — put the malignant centroids tighter than the null (Note S-K). This is the size of the effect: unrelated cancers do occupy a common region, but the headline is a shared high-output state, with a smaller specific-shape residue on top. The convergence does not depend on which normal cell we call each cancer's origin — the origin labels are coarse and used only to annotate lineage (Table S8) — and the four added-lineage cohorts whose malignant cells are marker-labeled rather than author-annotated (both sarcomas, both germ-cell tumors) — where marker selection (POU5F1/SALL4 in the germ-cell tumors) risks picking the very stem/high-output cells that then converge — can be dropped with the result essentially unchanged (SI). The convergence itself is present in 24 of 25 cancers.

### Where the convergence lands: a reproducible neighbourhood, and the cell nearest it under this catalog

Where the cancers land, and whether their target survives scrutiny, are separate questions (Fig. 3). To name the target without circularity we rank all 154 normal cell types not by proximity to any pre-chosen point but by **cross-cancer recurrence** — the number of cancers in which a cell is among the five most frequent nearest neighbours — and, orthogonally, by depth (the *median* per-cancer fraction of cells reaching it; the mean is reported alongside it in Table S2, and on the mean the third-ranked candidate — the gastric progenitor, 10.4% — is not separated from the cytotrophoblast at 11.0%, while the megakaryocyte leads at 13.5%). Both rankings are led, by a clear margin, by two cells: the **cytotrophoblast** (a top target in 18 of 25 cancers) and the **megakaryocyte** (18 of 25), with no clean third. The two are separated by a transcriptome-complexity control (Fig. 3D): as detected-gene count rises, cytotrophoblast assignment *rises* while megakaryocyte assignment *falls*, tracking the nucleus-less erythrocyte we quarantine as a low-complexity artifact. Under this catalog, therefore, the cytotrophoblast is the cell nearest the region — the highest-depth, complexity-robust target, and the one normal cell that natively invades tissue, proliferates and evades immunity — and the megakaryocyte is carried no further than a secondary target. We state this as a property of the ranking under our catalog, not as an identification of the state. Four lines of evidence set that scope. Three independently reconstructed catalogs reproduce the region and name a different cell inside it (below). Within our own catalog the same fragility is visible gene by gene: four single genes — EIF4EBP1, FKBP5, SLC2A1 and CD36 — each displace the cytotrophoblast from the top two when removed alone (Note S-O-iv). Re-scoring the same modules by regulatory output rather than component abundance keeps the convergence while moving the target to another cell (Note S-T). And repairing three gene symbols the catalog had been scoring wrongly and re-deriving the reference, the eigenspace and all 25 cohorts end to end leaves the region, the two leading cells and the within-patient effect in place — but the corrected catalog's own Kaiser criterion selects ten dimensions rather than nine, and on ten the cytotrophoblast leaves the top two (Note S-W). Nothing in §§5–7 depends on the name. It is not set aside for being blood-specific — it draws a comparable share of convergence in several solid cancers (germ-cell 29%, renal 19%, breast 14%) as in the hematopoietic ones — but because it fails the complexity control, falling with detected-gene count exactly as the quarantined erythrocyte does (Fig. 3D, Fig. S5-II). Those three properties describe the *normal* cell, and only the first two appear to be inherited: malignant cells in the state carry the invasion program (MMP2, MMP9, ITGA5) but show no detectable relation between depth in the state and immune infiltration — which is what the coordinate itself predicts, since Type I interferon is the one target-defining module cancer does *not* reproduce (below; SI). Convergence onto the anchor alone captures **11.1% of malignant cells, 16.9-fold** over the 0.66% expected for a single cell type. We lead with this rather than with the two-cell core, whose 23.9% (18.2-fold over 1.3%) is about half contributed by the megakaryocyte that the complexity control in Fig. 3D rejects. The enrichment persists as the region is widened (15.9-fold for the four most recurrent cell types, 8.4-fold for the fourteen; Fig. 3F, Table 1), and survives applying the complexity control to every member rather than only to the two leading ones: six of the fourteen fall with transcriptome complexity as the quarantined erythrocyte does, but dropping them leaves the enrichment essentially unchanged, because the chance baseline falls with the membership (Note S-Q); the convergent cells form a genuine neighbourhood (mutual cosine 0.77 versus −0.01 for random pairs).

The naming survives the obvious alternatives. It is **not the geometric centre** of the normal manifold, which any contracting process would reach: the true medoid — an alveolar type-1 pneumocyte — receives *zero* convergence (outside the top 70 of 154; many cells tie at the 0.04% floor, so the exact rank is tie-rule dependent), and the malignant attractor points away from the centre and onto the core. It is **not the germline**, which matches the core on the single most heavily weighted axis but diverges across the remaining eight, stable across 2,000 bootstraps (SI). And it is **not a technical artifact**: the one cell outranking the core on raw proximity, the erythrocyte, is a nucleus-less low-complexity artifact quarantined in quality control by the same control that clears the cytotrophoblast. One structural caveat belongs here rather than in a note. The trophoblast states in the reference come from a different dataset and platform (Vento-Tormo et al., 2018) than the remaining 154-type gallery, so a platform contribution to their position cannot be excluded; because those states also carry most of the within-patient signal in §5, this is a limitation of the paper and not only of §3, and we flag it as such (Note S-K, Discussion). Nor does the result depend on the exact module *partition*: dropping any five modules and rebuilding the whole pipeline leaves the convergence and the ranking essentially unchanged, and dropping each of the 25 cohorts in turn leaves the cytotrophoblast ranked first in eight panels and second in the remaining seventeen. Adding five decoy modules of random genes was pre-registered as the harsher test, and measured against the right comparator it is not harsher: in the reduced harness where a decoy module can be scored at all, the cytotrophoblast holds the top three in 42% of decoy draws against 48% when five *real* modules are dropped under the same metric and the same harness (Fisher *P* = 0.39), and the core fraction and its enrichment are marginally *higher* with the decoys present. The two percentages that look discrepant are two rankings and not two perturbations: by cosine to the malignant attractor, where the cytotrophoblast is first unperturbed, it stays in the top three in 84% of five-module drops; by cross-cancer recurrence, where it is third unperturbed, in 44% — which is what moving a third-ranked cell by a fifth of the catalog does. No decoy arm exists in the full 25-cohort pipeline, because a decoy module has to be scored gene by gene and only the reduced harness does that (Note S-O(iv)). What the partition control does *not* remove is the curated gene list itself: reshuffling the same 203 scored genes into random modules still yields 14% convergence against 6% for random genome-wide panels and 21% for the real modules. Above the random-genome floor that is about half the effect carried by the gene set rather than by how those genes are grouped, and the real-versus-reshuffled difference is not itself significant (z = +1.3); the gene set was assembled with cancer biology in view, and we treat that prior as unresolved rather than bounded. The gene-level reproduction and the module-drop control bound that residual prior; they do not erase it. How far does the *naming* depend on this particular catalog? We tested that directly, by asking three independent curations to build a module catalog from the same four criteria and the same size target, with our catalog, this paper and the words "trophoblast" and "megakaryocyte" withheld. The answer separates two claims that are easy to conflate. The convergence *region* reproduces: five normal cell types — gastric progenitor, gastric chief, pancreatic acinar, oesophageal apical and the megakaryocyte — are among the ten nearest targets under all four catalogs, and every catalog lands on the same neighbourhood of high-output secretory and progenitor states. The *anchor within that region* does not: each independent curation shares about half our genes, and all three place the cytotrophoblast 26th to 35th of 152 by proximity (cosine 0.51–0.64 against 0.96 for their own winner) and name the gastric progenitor instead. The four criteria therefore constrain the region but do not by themselves select the cell inside it; the cytotrophoblast is the anchor *under this catalog*, supported by the depth and complexity controls above and by the orthogonal signature below, and we state it at that strength (Note S-O).

This is a lineage-independent convergence onto one region of normal regulatory space — distinct from the lineage-*restricted* convergence onto an organ-matched fetal cell reported within a single cancer type (Kildisiute et al., 2021), and from cancer-derived recurrent-program catalogues (Barkley et al., 2022) in locating a convergent *normal* destination rather than a recurrent malignant program.

### A reduced negative-feedback transcriptional signature defines the convergent state

A final test closes the circle on non-circularity (Fig. 4). Setting cancer aside and asking which of the 44 modules single the core out among all 154 normal cells, the answer is defined with no cancer data: the target cells are the pair that carry a markedly reduced transcriptional signature of a dozen negative-feedback and checkpoint modules — Circadian, Type I IFN, JAK-STAT, PI3K/PTEN, Notch, Wnt, SREBP and others — sitting ~1–1.8 standard deviations below the rest of the panel on each; Fig. S8 shows the core against the full normal-cell distribution for every module, so the reader can see which of the 44 the claim rests on. Two cautions apply to every statement of this kind in this paper. First, a module score is a **transcriptional signature, not a biochemical measurement of pathway activity**: reduced expression of a negative-feedback gene is consistent with release of that feedback, but equally with the pathway being less active overall, so we speak throughout of a reduced or enhanced negative-feedback *signature* and not of released inhibition. Second, the readout is only as good as the genes actually scored — 104 of the 216 scored gene slots (Table S9 lists 219 slots over 206 unique genes; the three remaining slots are the deprecated aliases `ARNTL`, `MB21D1` and `TMEM173`, which matched nothing in the reference and so were scored by no cell in the numbers reported here; the re-derivation that repairs them is Note S-W) are induced genes (feedback elements or transcription-factor targets), while six modules (AMPK, Hippo, Insulin/FOXO, Calcium, cAMP/CREB, NPAS4) score none at all and are read through their components alone (Table S9b, Note S-N). Scoring the same modules by regulatory *output* instead of component *abundance* was pre-registered as a test of the right measurement level, and it separates the two halves of the result. The convergence is *larger* at the output level — 33% of malignant cells in the three-cell core against 25% under readiness scoring in the same harness — and the magnitude-removed within-patient deepening three to four times larger (+19.5 points against +4.9). On raw scores the within-patient arm goes the other way, and we state it: the output-level deepening is +1.5 points with 26 of 46 patients higher, against +13.9 and 43 of 46 under readiness scoring, so it is the *magnitude-removed* comparison and not both that favours the output level (Table 2). And the cytotrophoblast is not the cell converged upon. It falls to rank 17 of 152 with 0 of 25 cancers under the pre-registered estimator, and the output-level target is the megakaryocyte in 25 of 25. The pre-registration committed the test to fail on exactly that, and we report it as a failure: what this catalog reads when it names the cytotrophoblast is a cell's *equipment* for the state rather than its pathway output (Note S-T).

### The convergent state exists in normal physiology but malignancy increases its occupancy

The natural reading — that this is a cancer signature — is wrong, and the correction is the pivot of the paper (Fig. 5A). Running the identical pipeline on healthy tissue, normal lung epithelium occupies the convergent state — defined throughout as a cell whose nearest normal neighbour is one of three reference types, the cytotrophoblast, the migrating cytotrophoblast and the megakaryocyte (Methods; Note S-K) — at 16.1% — as fully as many of the cancers — while healthy kidney (0.5%) and neural tissue (7.6%) sit far from it. Proximity to the convergent state is therefore a **tissue-baseline property of normal physiology**, not a destination unique to cancer: the professional secretory and high-output epithelia constitutively occupy it, transport and post-mitotic tissues do not. The unit of the result is therefore a physiological *cell state*. The question is no longer where cancer goes, but what it does once there.

Holding tissue baseline fixed answers it (Fig. 5B). Because a cancer arises from a particular epithelial subtype rather than from a tissue, the comparison must be to the right normal cell. Whole-transcriptome similarity identifies it: scored on 2,000 highly-variable genes rather than on the modules, the malignant epithelium of 57 of 62 patients is closest to the proliferating crypt-base stem/transit-amplifying population, and each malignant subtype is closest to its own normal counterpart. Comparing like with like does not remove the effect: against normal crypt-base progenitors specifically, malignant epithelium is still higher in 35 of 39 patients (+13.6 points), and the same subtype against itself gives +14.8 points. Comparing instead against the differentiated surface epithelium, which occupies the state far less often, would have overstated the effect, so the crypt-base comparison is the primary one (Note S-O). In a colorectal cohort with paired tumor and adjacent-normal tissue (Pelka et al., 2021), malignant epithelium occupies the core at a median 18.9% versus 4.7% for the *same patient's* normal colon epithelium — an increase of about sixteen percentage points on the per-patient mean, higher in 43 of 46 patients (a fourfold ratio of the medians, but we report points because the fold-change is inflated by the small normal denominator) (paired *P* = 9 × 10⁻¹³). Applied to every further tissue with paired single-cell data, malignant epithelium occupies the state more often than the same patient's matched-normal. Where the baseline is low the increase is large: an independent colorectal dataset replicates it (23/25 donors), and kidney and liver concur (each individually underpowered but same-signed). Pooled across these four datasets, malignant epithelium is deeper in **77 of 86 patients**; because pooling patients across datasets could manufacture a Simpson's-paradox artifact, a mixed-effects model that credits the dataset clustering — a dataset random intercept — estimates a **+13.0-percentage-point increase in occupancy (SE 1.87; z = 6.95; one-sided *P* = 1.9 × 10⁻¹²)**, with all four datasets increasing in the mean. The effect is not a tumor-versus-normal complexity artifact: resampling malignant cells to each patient's own matched-normal complexity distribution retains about 80% of it (SI). A second organ replicates it: twenty-three paired gastric patients (GSE150290), analyzed only after the account was fixed (**18/22, 8.8% → 14.2%, *P* = 0.001; Fig. S11-I**). None of the paired cohorts is cleanly outside the panel: the panel’s gastric cohort is these same twenty-three tumour samples, the panel’s colorectal cohort *is* Pelka, and five of the ten paired lung patients are among the nine GSE131907 donors carried by the panel’s smallest cohort (Note S-U, `scripts/verify/paired_overlap.py`). What the paired design buys is not independence of the patients but the cancellation of lineage and batch — both arms are the same patient, the same tissue, the same protocol — and the question it answers is whether malignancy raises occupancy of the state, not whether the state was named out of sample.

The increase is not gated by tissue baseline. An apparent absence in high-baseline lung was a cell-selection artifact: the lung "tumor" arm of the pan-tissue reference was ~90% normal-adjacent alveolar epithelium and only ~9% annotated-malignant cells, diluting it to the normal baseline. With cleanly identified malignant cells, high-baseline lung behaves like the rest (GSE131907: **10 of 10 patients, 23% → 39%, *P* = 0.002 — the smallest value a two-sided paired Wilcoxon can return at n = 10; Fig. S11-II**). Increased occupancy is therefore a **broadly shared feature of malignancy rather than a universal requirement**, not a low/high dichotomy: it holds in every tissue tested but one, high-baseline lung included, and the exceptions are real. Prostate is an open negative in which two paired cohorts disagree about the normal baseline itself and neither shows an increase (SI); the gastric cohort is the weakest of the three once magnitude is removed (+3.4 points, significant by Wilcoxon but only borderline by a sign test); and the pre-invasive prediction fails outright in Barrett’s oesophagus (below). We report these as limits on generality, not as noise. On the additive scale the amount is roughly constant across tissues (~+10 points; *r* = +0.4 with baseline, *P* = 0.5); the impression of "more room where the baseline is lower" is a fold-change artifact of a small denominator (SI).

Most important, the increase in occupancy is not merely the shared high output that carries the (modest) convergence. Because it is measured in the same core-fraction currency, it is exposed to the same magnitude confound, so we remove it: recomputing the core with the overall-magnitude component of each cell subtracted, patient-matched malignant epithelium still sits deeper in the **shape-only** core than matched-normal — colorectal +10.5 points (44/46; paired Wilcoxon *P* = 10⁻¹³, sign-test *P* < 10⁻¹⁰; the colorectal comparator recomputes the same quantity by its own route and gives +10.4 and 43/46, the single disagreeing patient being one whose two arms differ by 0.3 points, Note S-K), corrected lung +12.1 (9/10; *P* = 0.004, sign-test *P* = 0.011), and gastric +3.4 (15/22; *P* = 0.04 by Wilcoxon but only borderline by a sign test, *P* = 0.07) — retaining 63–77% of the raw effect (SI). The colorectal and lung shape-only results are robust under either test; the gastric cohort — the smallest — is the weakest of the three once magnitude is removed, and we mark it as suggestive rather than significant. What is specifically malignant is thus a genuine shift in regulatory *pattern* toward the target, not transcriptional brightness alone — and this within-patient increase in occupancy, not the cross-lineage convergence, is the strongest claim in this paper. It is also the most portable: established pathway collections put through the identical pipeline — KEGG, Reactome, Hallmark, PROGENy — reproduce the within-patient increase at the same size or larger, and under every one of them the largest within-patient shift in colorectal epithelium is toward a trophoblast state. The increase in occupancy is therefore a property of curated regulatory biology projected onto a normal-cell gallery, not of this catalog in particular; what this catalog adds is the cross-lineage generality of the target across all 25 cancers, which no off-the-shelf collection reproduces (Note S-O).

Correcting the lung reading cost a negative control, and we replace it with a stricter one: a *non-malignant disease of the same organ*. Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis is the hardest benign comparator available — chronically injured, ECM-remodelling epithelium containing the aberrant-basaloid and transitional-AT2 populations repeatedly described as cancer-like. If invasive remodelling alone sufficed, fibrotic lung should enter the state. It does not, or barely: across twenty-two donors, fibrotic lung sits at **19.9% versus 16.1%** for control lung (raw) and **8.9% versus 7.6%** (shape-only) — rises of 3.8 and 1.3 points, neither significant (one-sided unpaired Mann–Whitney over the donors, *P* = 0.12 and 0.21; two-sided, 0.25 and 0.41) — whereas malignant epithelium in the same organ moves **23.4% → 39.0%** raw in ten of ten patients and **18.2% → 30.3%** shape-only in nine of ten (*P* = 0.002 and 0.004; Fig. S12), four- to ninefold more. The two sides are within-dataset effects from different cohorts, platforms and pipelines — the two normal-lung baselines themselves differ by 7 points (16.1% against 23.4%) — so this is a comparison of two within-dataset deltas, not a single contrast. Within fibrotic lung the ordering is biologically sensible (transitional-AT2 highest, ciliated lowest; SI), so the state tracks invasive high-output phenotypes generally — but only malignancy carries a tissue into it. The event also appears early and essentially all-or-none, independent of grade and stage (SI).

### Transformation of human cells from a known precursor moves them into the state

Every comparison so far matches a malignant cell to a normal cell we *believe* to be its counterpart. Transformation of human cells in culture removes the belief: the precursor is in the same dish, profiled before the oncogenic hits are delivered. We tested five isogenic series of this kind, each against a pre-registration frozen before the data were read (`perceptome_validation/PREREG_T9_invitro.md`). These are the only analyses in this paper that are not single-cell — an isogenic within-donor design exists in bulk RNA-seq and on microarray and nowhere else — so the coordinate is scored here on whole-population profiles, every statistic is a within-series paired difference, and arrays and sequencing are never pooled.

The displacement is large, and it arrives in two stages (Fig. 6A–C). In the one series carrying a paired within-donor time course — ten donors of primary human prostate basal cells transduced with five defined oncogenic factors and followed through to tumour formation (PARCB; Chen et al., 2023) — the cells travel +0.509 in cosine to the malignant attractor from their own untreated basal state to the endpoint tumour, and **71% of that distance (+0.360) is already covered at week 0**, in cells that carry all five factors and are not yet tumours (10 of 10 donors in both; sign-test *P* = 0.001), with the stage ordering monotone in every donor (mean Spearman ρ = +0.80). Scored by regulatory output instead of component abundance (DoRothEA/PROGENy footprints under VIPER), the same transduced-not-yet-tumour arm contributes nothing (−0.031; 1 of 10 donors) while the endpoint tumour still sits closer to the attractor than its own precursor (+0.052, 9 of 10, *P* = 0.011; magnitude-removed +0.028, 8 of 10, *P* = 0.055; under the ULM estimator +0.401 and +0.214, 10 of 10 for both). The discrete readout used throughout the main text — nearest-normal assignment — follows the output timing: no donor lies inside the convergent region at baseline, one after transduction, all ten at the endpoint tumour. The regulatory state therefore begins to move with the genetic lesion itself, before a malignant phenotype exists, and the part of the move visible at the level of pathway output accrues with the tumour. Both scorings are reported: they date the same displacement differently, and that difference is itself the result.

### Malignant cells superimpose stress and growth programs on the shared physiological state

Increased occupancy is only half of what malignancy does. The sharper question is what distinguishes a malignant cell *already in* the convergent state from a normal cell of the same patient that is also in it — same state, same tissue, same patient, so lineage and batch cancel (Fig. 7). Comparing the two occupant populations module by module within each of thirty colorectal patients (≥10 core-assigned cells per arm), and balancing detection depth so the comparison is not a library-size effect (Fig. 7), sixteen modules separate the occupants reproducibly. The malignant occupant carries higher transcriptional signatures of the retained integrity and stress responses — HIF, NRF2, HSF1, UPR-PERK and autophagy — together with anabolic and proliferative ones (mTOR, SREBP, cell cycle, Wnt), and lower signatures of PPARα, MR and PI3K/PTEN. For the kinase-centred modules in that list the caution of §4 applies with particular force: mTOR and PI3K/PTEN are scored almost entirely from component transcripts, so "higher mTOR" here means higher mTOR-component message and not demonstrated mTORC1 activity. The p53 module also rises, but through its downstream damage targets (p21, BAX, PUMA, NOXA) rather than *TP53* itself, whose transcript is unchanged — a replication-stress footprint rather than retained p53 function.

Critically, this difference lies largely *off* the axis that defines the target. Plotted against the reduced-feedback signature that distinguishes the target among normal cells, the malignant-versus-normal occupant differences correlate only weakly (ρ = +0.36): malignancy does not push cells further along the same axis that made the state special, it adds a different, stress-and-growth program on top of it (the module-by-module within-patient view is Fig. S15). The two statements should therefore be kept apart throughout: at the level of the *population*, malignancy increases occupancy of a pre-existing physiological state; among the cells *already occupying* it, malignancy modifies the state qualitatively rather than intensifying its defining features. This analysis is colorectal only and awaits replication wherever paired malignant and normal cells both occupy the state in sufficient numbers.

## Discussion

Read against Abelev's fifty-year-old embryonic-state theory of cancer (Abelev, 1971), the result gives the theory a concrete coordinate: cancer does not manufacture a program, it re-enters a bounded, pre-existing normal one — the physiology of the invasive, proliferative, high-output early placenta — and removes the bound. The convergence target is not unique to cancer, so the claim is a physiological state whose occupancy malignancy increases, not a cancer-specific destination; stated this way, the embryonic-state reading is strengthened rather than weakened, because the destination demonstrably pre-exists in normal biology, and the coordinate system that reveals it was built from normal cells alone.

This positions the work among, and apart from, several literatures. The closest design, a bulk projection of tumors onto a normal-cell space reporting one destination (Chen & He, 2016), landed on an abstract embryonic-stem state; resolving single malignant cells across twenty-five lineages lands instead on a specific, differentiated region of normal state. Pan-cancer stemness indices (Malta et al., 2018) and developmental-potency scores (Gulati et al., 2020) measure a scalar toward a generic pluripotent reference; the convergent region is genuinely high-potency by those measures, but here it resolves to a bounded region of normal states rather than a "more stem-like" number. The result also engages the contrasting lineage-local view (Patel & Yanai, 2024): the cross-lineage clustering reported here is the direct test, reconciled by noting that convergence is a claim about the *destination*, not the *direction* of travel — origins as different as blood and skin reach the same region without following the same path. The trophoblast–cancer resemblance, long argued qualitatively (Ferretti et al., 2007; Costanzo et al., 2018), becomes here a measured, positional, cross-lineage claim — about one region, at one level of measurement and under one module catalog.

We are explicit about what the data do and do not support. A convergent endpoint in static snapshots is not a proven dynamical attractor: the coordinate system locates where cancers come to rest, but attraction is a claim about motion (Kauffman, 1971; Huang et al., 2009). One time-resolved dataset lets us take a first step. Following human lung-adenocarcinoma cells through eleven days of targeted therapy (Aissa et al., 2021), the population moves in the coordinate: occupancy rises sharply by day 2, drops at day 4 as the drug-tolerant persister trough forms, and recovers through days 9–11 as the survivors resume growth. We report the shape of that course and not its levels, because the levels are a function of the quality-control threshold — day 2 reads anywhere from 18% to 62% as the detected-gene floor moves from 0 to 1,000 — and because core assignment in this shallow Drop-seq data tracks transcriptome complexity within every timepoint (Spearman +0.77 to +1.00). Restricting all timepoints to a common detected-gene window leaves the rise and the trough intact, so the movement is not a complexity artifact (Fig. S14); whether the population ends *above* where it began is window-dependent and we make no such claim. A cell line without a microenvironment, with survivorship thinning the later timepoints and no lineage tracing, also cannot separate movement of cells from selection among them. Notably, the state is not moved by *single-gene* perturbation at all: across nearly ten thousand CRISPR knockdowns, knocking a target-defining brake down shifts its own module by up to two standard deviations yet leaves the nine-dimensional position essentially unchanged and in no consistent direction (SI). The state behaves as a many-module consensus — buffered against single genes, displaced by coordinated pressure. Four candidate organizing principles for the state — driver-gene enrichment, module evolutionary age, an exteroception–interoception axis, and immune privilege — were each tested and falsified (SI). One further observation, which we report as suggestive rather than established, sharpens what the state is *for*. Comparing each patient's primary tumour with their own lymph-node metastasis, the metastasis sits **lower** in the state, not higher — in breast, means of 21.8% → 13.4% across six patients (lower in six of six; paired *P* = 0.031, which is the floor a two-sided Wilcoxon can reach at n = 6; shape-only 14.3% → 9.6%), with the single evaluable liver pair in the same direction. Of the two portal-vein tumour thrombi available — tumour actively occupying a vessel — one sits well above its primary and the other does not move, so we read them as a single suggestive case. Read together, the state accompanies invasion and primary growth and is relinquished on colonization, which is what the trophoblast analogy would predict: the cytotrophoblast lineage invades the decidua, it does not seed distant organs. The comparison rests on nodal rather than distant metastases and on small numbers, and needs a dedicated cohort.

The account makes falsifiable predictions: pre-invasive lesions should already occupy the convergent region before invasion is histologically present, and we have now tested this in three organs with a mixed result we report in full (Fig. S13-I, Fig. S13-II). In colorectum it holds: across three patients with matched normal, adenoma and carcinoma epithelium, the adenoma already sat above matched normal in all three, at roughly two-thirds of the carcinoma level. In breast it holds directionally: ductal carcinoma *in situ* sits between normal breast and invasive carcinoma (7.7% → 11.3% → 21.8%), reaching about half the invasive level, though with a single normal sample the comparison is weak. In esophagus it fails: Barrett's metaplasia sits *below* the same patient's own normal squamous epithelium in four of four patients (2.1% versus 7.7%) and below gastric epithelium in three of three. Two readings are open and we do not choose between them: either the prediction is confined to *neoplastic* precursors — the adenoma and the in-situ carcinoma are clonal neoplasms, Barrett's metaplasia is a lineage switch and a risk factor rather than a neoplasm — or the prediction is simply wrong in esophagus. The datasets available to us are mostly non-dysplastic Barrett's, so the properly pre-invasive esophageal lesion, dysplasia, remains untested. A second prediction: cancers adjacent to the core should not reproduce the one trophoblast brake — Type I IFN silencing — that defines the target but that cancer does not follow. (An earlier structural argument — that the two core cells share polyploidy by endoreduplication — we drop as imprecise: the megakaryocyte does endoreduplicate, but the villous cytotrophoblast named here as the anchor is the trophoblast lineage's mononuclear diploid progenitor; polyploidy is a property of its syncytial and extravillous derivatives, not of the anchor cell itself.) The within-patient occupancy test is released as a dataset-agnostic pipeline that, given any paired tumor/normal cohort, predicts increased occupancy — with no condition on the tissue's baseline — the direct replication the generality of the claim invites.

Four controls bound the claim and change how the result should be read (Notes S-A, S-B, S-C and S-H). The convergence is substantially a signal of overall regulatory *level*: removing the magnitude component and keeping only the relative module shape reduces the core convergence from ~27% to ~7–11% (still four- to fivefold over chance), so the reading is convergence onto a *high-output* state carried largely by module magnitude, with a smaller but real module-shape residual. Magnitude does not, however, explain *which* normal cell is named, and three orthogonal tests say the address is specific rather than merely bright: removing magnitude *sharpens* the target onto the trophoblast instead of diffusing it across high-output cells; the professional high-output secretory cells a pure-magnitude account predicts — hepatocytes and plasma cells — each receive ~0.1% of the cross-cancer convergence against 11–13% for the core; and core assignment is essentially uncorrelated with library size and detected-gene count. The magnitude of the effect is calibration-dependent, resolved only under the single-cell-matched calibration the single-cell tumour data require — a choice justified *without cancer*, because the pseudobulk alternative collapses even independent *normal* single cells onto low-complexity targets (Note S-H). And "no cancer data" is precise about the coordinate system and not the feature set: the 44 modules were curated from regulatory biology, some of it studied in cancer, a residual prior that the module-drop robustness (Fig. S7-II) and the module-independent gene-level reproduction (Fig. S7-III) bound but do not erase.

Four limitations attach specifically to the *identity*, which we hold more provisionally than the convergence itself. The 154-cell-type gallery is a limited sample of normal states, sparse in progenitor and fetal cells, so the cytotrophoblast is most defensibly read as the nearest *available* normal proxy for an invasive, high-output state; a richer developmental atlas could refine the target. The trophoblast reference comes from another dataset and platform (§3), a difference the shape-only result bounds but does not erase. And the name is specific to the level at which the modules are measured: scored by regulatory output rather than by component abundance, the convergence survives and grows while the cytotrophoblast leaves the top ten entirely, so the identity claim is about a cell's equipment for the state rather than about its pathway output (Note S-T). And it is specific to the dimension of the frame: re-deriving the catalog with three repaired gene symbols leaves the region and the two leading cells in place on nine dimensions, but the corrected reference's own Kaiser criterion selects ten, and on ten the cytotrophoblast is not among the two most recurrent cells (Note S-W). Against the first two, one external check argues the address is not a gallery artifact: applied to mouse fibroblast-to-iPSC reprogramming (Schiebinger et al., 2019) — another species, another laboratory, no cancer anywhere in the analysis — the fraction of cells the coordinate assigns to the cytotrophoblast collapses to 1.8% early in reprogramming and then rises about ninefold, to between 11% and 20% from day fourteen onward (median 16.7%; ρ = +0.76 with reprogramming day, *P* = 3 × 10⁻⁸; Fig. S9), which is the window in which this system is known to throw off extraembryonic and trophoblast-like fates. Two caveats travel with it, and both are material: the starting fibroblast timepoints score anomalously high and we cannot explain it — if unreprogrammed fibroblasts already score high, the coordinate may be reading proliferative high-output character rather than trophoblast identity and the collapse-then-rise could be a composition effect — and the dataset carries no cell-type labels, so we cannot confirm these are the same cells the original study called trophoblast-like. To map the remaining analytic freedom rather than assert a point in it, a 36-way specification curve crossing calibration, magnitude handling, core definition and erythroid treatment puts the convergence above chance in every one of the eighteen specifications run under the single-cell calibration; the only specifications that fall to chance are confined to the pseudobulk calibration we independently show to degenerate (Fig. S10).

## Methods (summary; full procedures in SI)

**Modules and eigenspace.** 44 modules curated with the four operational criteria in view — the criteria record how the panel was built and are not a verified property of the genes as scored (Table S9b, Note S-N); module signature scored as mean log-normalized expression of component genes across 154 Human Protein Atlas single-cell types (Uhlén et al., 2015; trophoblast reference states from Vento-Tormo et al., 2018), z-scored and decomposed by correlation PCA, nine components retained by the Kaiser criterion. **Cancer projection.** Per cancer, malignant single cells are quality-filtered, scored on the 44 modules, projected through the fixed loadings, and assigned to their top-1 nearest normal cell-type centroid by cosine. **Non-circular target identification.** Cross-cancer recurrence (top-5 membership over the 25 cancers) and per-cancer depth, computed after quarantining the erythrocyte-family low-complexity artifact. **Cross-origin contraction.** Mean pairwise cosine distance among the 25 malignant centroids against a matched-averaging null — 200–300 aggregates of unrelated normal cells formed by the same averaging and of the same sizes — reported raw and with the overall-magnitude component removed. The earlier comparison against random 25-cell-type draws (a Stayton-type test; Stayton, 2015) is superseded: it mis-specified the null by ignoring the averaging itself, and the fold-contraction it produced is withdrawn (Note S-G). **Within-patient occupancy** (the study's falsifiable general assay). For each cohort with paired tumor and adjacent-normal epithelium of the same patients (Pelka et al., 2021; Lu Y et al., 2022; CELLxGENE Census), occupancy of the convergent state — a cell whose top-1 nearest normal neighbour among the 152 candidates is the cytotrophoblast, the migrating cytotrophoblast or the megakaryocyte, chance 3/152 = 2.0% — is computed per patient for the malignant and matched-normal arms (≥ 20 cells each) and compared by paired Wilcoxon and a sign test; the unit of replication is the patient, not the cell. **Cell-count denominators.** Four different totals appear in this paper and each means something different: the full quality-controlled pool across the 25 cohorts is 448,399 cells (Table S1); the subset carried into the projection after the ≥1,000-detected-gene filter but *before* the 4,000-cells-per-cohort cap is 312,220 cells — drawn from the 17 of the 25 cohorts whose objects retain per-cell detected-gene counts, which is the pool the complexity analyses use for that reason (Fig. S5-II, Note S-Q); applying the cap leaves the 83,819 cells on which every convergence statistic is computed; and Table S6 tabulates 574,451 assignments because it includes the normal-atlas projections as well as the cancers. **Reproducibility.** The pipeline is released with the 44 module gene lists (Table S7), the exact CELLxGENE Census snapshot used throughout (version 2025-11-08), all GEO accessions (Data Availability), and a runnable end-to-end script that regenerates the main and SI figures from public inputs (Code availability). Full parameters, quality control, controls, and the single-cell-only rationale are in SI.

## Note S1 — Full methods

**Modules.** A regulatory module is defined by four operational criteria: a sensor input; a transcription-factor output with documented targets; a negative-feedback target enabling cyclical operation; and receptor-field specificity. Screening ~75 candidate signaling and metabolic axes (KEGG, Reactome, primary literature) against these criteria yielded 44 modules. Module activity is scored as the mean log-normalized expression of component genes.

**Eigenspace.** The 154 × 44 Human Protein Atlas single-cell matrix (Uhlén et al., 2015; scRNA-seq data, not protein measurements) was z-scored per module and decomposed by PCA. Nine components were retained by the Kaiser criterion (73.8% of variance). Bootstrap resampling with replacement (n = 154, 500 iterations) gives eigenvector stability that falls monotonically with the axis: 0.75–0.99 for PC1–PC6 and 0.53–0.71 for PC7–PC9 (Fig. S1-I). The 100-iteration field stored with the reference object (0.63–0.99 and 0.40–0.54, with PC8 below PC9) does not reproduce under this recipe and is withdrawn; no code for it survives.

**Two HPA calibrations.** The eigenspace is built on the *pseudobulk* HPA reference. For per-cell tumor projection, the reference is re-derived under the *single_cell_scaled* calibration — the same `normalize_total(1e4)+log1p` preprocessing applied to the tumor data — so per-cell z-scores are commensurate; projecting under the pseudobulk calibration collapses tumor cells to low-complexity targets (scale mismatch). All cancer projections use single_cell_scaled.

**Cancer projection.** Per cancer: load malignant cells; remap ENSEMBL→symbol where needed; uppercase and de-duplicate gene names; quality-filter to n_genes ≥ 1000; `normalize_total(1e4)+log1p` if max > 100; score 44 modules (`mean_raw`); z-score against the single_cell_scaled reference; project through the 44×9 loading matrix; record the top-1 nearest of 154 cell-type centroids (cosine).

**Convergence projection (Fig. S4-I).** Malignant cells of two contrasting cancers (B-ALL, neuroblastoma; ≤2,000 cells each, n_genes ≥ 1000) are projected onto two interpretable 9-D directions — cosine to the core centroid (convergence axis) and cosine to a neural-program centroid (brain excitatory/inhibitory neurons and adrenal medulla cells — the neuronal/sympathoadrenal program neuroblastoma derives from; neural-program axis) — with 20/50/80% KDE density contours over the 154 normal cell types. B-ALL median core-cosine 0.86 (neural-program 0.07); neuroblastoma 0.59 (neural-program 0.47). Positions are static snapshots, not trajectories. These axes are used because the raw PC1–PC2 projection does not resolve the convergence (below).

**Convergence map (Fig. S4-II).** The 154 normal cell types are colored by the mean across 25 cancers of the fraction of malignant cells assigned to each (top-1), painted on the PC1–PC2 layout. Color carries the convergence statistic; position is eigenspace location, shown for context only. A raw projection of per-cell tumor coordinates into PC1–PC2 and re-derivation of nearest-neighbor assignment recovers only 8.2% core-assignment for B-ALL against 60.8% in the full 9-D space, and 6.8% against 23.9% pooled over all 25 cancers; any two cancers' raw clouds overlap across the whole manifold — convergence is a high-dimensional nearest-neighbor property, not a 2-D location. (Both arms of each pair are recomputed on the current 83,819-cell panel under the single quarantine convention; `scripts/E42_2d_vs_9d.py`.)

**Target identification (non-circular).** Cell types are ranked by cross-cancer *recurrence* (number of the 25 cancers in which the cell is among the five most frequent top-1 assignments), not by proximity to a pre-chosen centroid. The two-cell core (megakaryocyte, cytotrophoblast) leads this ranking with a clear margin (cytotrophoblast 18/25, megakaryocyte 18/25) and is independently the only pair leading the *median per-cancer fraction* (depth) ranking. No third cell leads on both metrics (Table S2; Fig. S6-I). The erythrocyte and platelet — post-cellular bodies — are excluded (Note S3); the erythrocyte is also the documented low-complexity artifact (Fig. S5-I/S4).

**Enrichment.** Reported as fold-enrichment over the k/152 chance baseline (152 candidates after the erythrocyte/platelet quarantine; Note S-K) across three principled boundaries (top-2 core, 18.2×; top-4 neighbourhood, 15.9×; top-14 broader region, 8.4×) to show boundary-independence: boundaries are defined by rank in the cross-cancer recurrence ranking, and all three are computed on the same 83,819-cell panel: the top-2 core captures 23.9% of malignant cells (chance 2/152 = 1.32%, 18.2×), the top-4 neighbourhood 41.9% (chance 2.63%, 15.9×) and the top-14 broader region 76.9% (chance 9.21%, 8.4×). Region coherence is the mean pairwise eigenspace cosine among the convergent cells (0.77 vs −0.01 for random pairs).

**Progression.** Within-cancer, patient-level aggregation with n_genes control; HCC clinical stage (Lu 2022) and colorectal grade/T-stage/nodal status (Pelka 2021); Table S5.

**Convergence contraction (Fig. 2).** Each cancer's malignant 9-D centroid (mean over QC-passing malignant cells) is paired with its normal cell of origin in the 154 reference (e.g. HCC→hepatocyte, B-ALL→B-cell, osteosarcoma→fibroblast, seminoma→spermatogonia; full map in Table S8). The contraction is the ratio of the mean pairwise cosine distance among the 25 origins to that among the 25 malignant centroids. Significance is **not** taken against 2,000 random draws of 25 normal cell types (a Stayton-type convergence test): that null is **superseded**, because it ignores the averaging itself, and the fold-contraction it produced is withdrawn. The test that stands is the matched-averaging null of coherent normal micro-populations, which is what the main text and Fig. 2 report — see **Note S-G**, which is the Methods statement for this figure. A replicator should read Note S-G before reproducing this panel. The MDS basin (Fig. S4-II) is a metric-MDS embedding of the origins + malignant centroids on their full 9-D distance matrix (all axes, not leading PCs).

**Panel expansion to 25.** Six datasets were added to span origin classes absent from the original nineteen: small-cell lung (neuroendocrine) and gastric (Census, malignant = epithelial compartment); osteosarcoma (GSE162454) and Ewing sarcoma (GSE130024, PDX) for mesenchymal origin; embryonal germ-cell tumor (GSE228501) and seminoma (GSE197778) for germ-cell origin. For GEO datasets without author malignant annotations, malignant cells were identified by Leiden clustering and lineage markers (osteoblastic SATB2/RUNX2/COL1A1; Ewing CD99/NKX2-2; germ-cell POU5F1/SALL4/KIT) versus immune (PTPRC). Per-dataset detail in Table S1.

**Geometry / coordinate invariance.** Convergent-region compactness was tested in three coordinate systems — PCA (9-D), varimax-rotated (6-D), and diffusion-map (8-D) — against a null of random equal-size cell subsets (Note S4, Table S3).

**Disease negative control and within-patient matched test (Fig. 4; Figs. S11-II, S12).** The identical pipeline (QC n_genes ≥ 1000 → normalize_total(1e4)+log1p → 44-module mean_raw scoring → z vs single_cell_scaled HPA → 9-PC projection → top-1 nearest of 154) was first validated to reproduce the cancer core fractions (Gastric 16.5% vs panel 16.1%; AML 20.4% vs 21.3%), then applied to four chronic-disease atlases with matched-normal arms: rheumatoid arthritis (excluded — median 286 genes/cell, below the QC floor), idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (Habermann 2020), diabetic kidney disease, and Alzheimer's (Grubman 2019). Core fraction was computed per arm with the erythrocyte low-complexity default reported separately; tissue baselines: kidney 0.5–1.0%, neural 7.6%, lung epithelium 16.1% (Control) / 19.9% (IPF) on the raw core and 7.6% / 8.9% shape-only (22 donors; Fig. S12), breast epithelium 25% (Wu 2021). For the within-patient test, malignant vs patient-matched normal epithelium of the *same* tissue was compared per patient (≥ 20 cells/arm) by paired Wilcoxon plus a sign test, across every tissue with paired tumour-and-adjacent-normal single-cell data. Low-baseline tissues: Pelka colorectal (46 patients, malignant median 18.9% vs normal 4.7%, 43/46, *P* = 9 × 10⁻¹³; separate normal samples, clean); an independent colorectal cohort (Human Tumor Atlas via Census, 23/25, one-sided *P* = 2 × 10⁻⁶); kidney (clear-cell RCC, Census, 6/7, median 11.5% vs 5.3%, *P* = 0.055); and liver (Lu HCC — 8 of its 10 patients have ≥ 20 hepatocytes in *both* the tumour and adjacent-normal arms, distinct from the 10-patient stage-trend analysis in the main text which only requires tumour-side stage metadata — 5/8, 19.4% vs 10.9%, *P* = 0.074). Pooled across these 86 patients, 77 are deeper (sign-test *P* = 7 × 10⁻¹⁵); a mixed-effects model with a dataset random intercept, which credits the between-dataset clustering, gives +13.0 points (SE 1.87, *z* = 6.95, one-sided *P* = 2 × 10⁻¹²). **Retracted negative control.** An earlier version of this work reported lung adenocarcinoma (Census, 2/8, median 14% for the “tumour” arm against 27% for the patient-matched adjacent-normal arm of the same cohort) as a high-baseline tissue showing no deepening. Three different normal lung values appear in this paper and they are three different things: 26.7% is that cohort's *patient-matched adjacent normal*, 11.7% is a *pool of healthy Census donors* (Fig. S11-IIB), and 23.4% is the *matched normal arm of the GSE131907 cohort* (Fig. S11-IIA). That reading is withdrawn: the Census "tumour" arm assembled by the broad epithelial filter is only ~9% annotated-malignant cells and ~91% normal-adjacent alveolar epithelium, so it was diluted to the normal baseline. With cleanly identified malignant cells (GSE131907) lung deepens like every other tissue tested — 23.4% → 39.0% raw in 10 of 10 patients (*P* = 0.002) and 18.2% → 30.3% shape-only in 9 of 10 (*P* = 0.004) (Fig. S11-II, Note S-J). No baseline-gating claim is made anywhere in this paper. Wu BRCA is uninformative (its "normal epithelial" cells are intratumoral, not separate adjacent-normal), and pooled cross-dataset ratios are vulnerable to Simpson's paradox (Lu pooled 4.6× collapses per patient); only the within-patient paired test is reported as causal. **Prostate — an unresolved tissue, reported as such.** In a 15-patient paired tumour/adjacent-benign atlas (CELLxGENE) prostate shows no within-patient deepening (7 of 15 patients). We previously set it aside as a saturated high-baseline tissue (~15%); that justification rested on the baseline-gating hypothesis we now retract, so we do not use it. Re-analysing an independent paired prostate cohort (Song 2022, GSE176031, four patients with paired benign and tumour epithelium) does not resolve it either: there the benign prostate baseline is *low* (2.1%, near the chance level) and the tumour arm reaches only 3.8% (paired *P* = 0.38, n.s.). The two prostate datasets therefore disagree about the baseline itself, and neither shows deepening. Prostate is counted as a tested tissue with a negative result, and the claim in the main text is stated accordingly as deepening in every tissue tested *except prostate*, whose cohorts are mutually inconsistent.

## Note S2 — NPAS4 leave-one-out

The single neuron-restricted module (NPAS4) does not shape the cross-tissue eigenspace. Rebuilding on 43 modules (pseudobulk basis, matching the canonical reference) reproduces all nine components with per-PC eigenvector cosine ≥ 0.95 (mean 0.99 over PC1–PC6), so all cross-tissue statistics transfer unchanged to the 44-module catalog. NPAS4 is retained for compatibility with neural single-cell datasets; its module score is near zero in non-neural cell types and does not affect cross-tissue analyses.

## Note S3 — Low-complexity artifact and quality control

Per-cell module scores depend on transcriptome complexity: cells with few detected genes carry degenerate profiles that map preferentially to low-complexity normal targets (erythrocytes median 890 genes, late spermatids 763 — both *below* the QC floor; Table S6). Screening 22 candidate cancer datasets by median genes/cell, five fall below 1,000 (AML 137, OralSCC 639, MM 665, Follicular 831, Gastric 912; Fig. S5-IA). These are excluded; AML and MM are replaced by GEO cohorts that pass the floor (van Galen 2019, Seq-Well, median ~1,050; Ledergor 2018, InDrops), the other three dropped. The final panel is 25 — nineteen passing the initial screen plus six added (two mesenchymal sarcomas, two germ-cell tumors, one neuroendocrine, one new epithelial tissue) to span all major origin classes (Note S1). The gastric dataset excluded here (median 912) is the original screen cohort; it is **not** the gastric in the final panel — that is a different CELLxGENE Census stomach dataset (the "one new epithelial tissue" added), whose twenty-three tumour donors are however the same twenty-three patients as the paired gastric test of §5, which is why that test is no longer described as held out (Note S-U), with median 1,169 genes/cell, above the floor (54% of its cells pass the per-cell n_genes ≥ 1000 filter). After QC, convergence-fraction shows no association with dataset cell count (Spearman ρ = +0.14, P = 0.60).

The erythrocyte family is excluded from the recurrence ranking and the main-panel figures specifically: before exclusion it ranks third (16 of 25), filling the gap between the core and the rest (Table S2 carries each cell's pre-quarantine occupancy; Note S-K). The core is not a residual artifact — megakaryocyte- and cytotrophoblast-assigned cells carry median 1,396 and 2,430 detected genes, well above the floor (Fig. S5-IB).

**Platform / label-quality.** Convergence-fraction ordering is sensitive to malignant-label quality (three cancers carry heuristic or Census labels; a colon dataset's author-validated relabeling moved it from 13% to 36%) and, despite uniform QC, to residual platform effects (one InDrops dataset; the rest 10x / Census / Seq-Well). Per-dataset provenance in Table S1.

**Marker-annotated additions do not drive the result.** Four of the six added cancers carry marker-based rather than author malignant labels (both sarcomas, both germ-cell tumors). Dropping all four and recomputing on the remaining 21 leaves the central numbers essentially unchanged: the malignant compartment's mean pairwise cosine distance is 0.166 across the full 25 and 0.174 across the 21 (the drop-4 value; not to be confused with the 0.174 obtained when only 25 cells per cancer are averaged, Fig. 2B) (both far below the 0.99 separating the tissues of origin), and the core holds rank 2 and 3 of 154 in the full-9-D cosine to the malignant attractor in both panels. The marker-based cancers neither create the convergence nor inflate the core's standing.

## Note S4 — Coordinate invariance attaches to the region, not the bare core

The convergent **region** (the four-cell majority-enriched neighborhood) is an unusually tight subset of the 154 cell types, and that tightness is coordinate-invariant: it is significant in both the linear PCA basis (percentile 0.005) and the varimax rotation (0.001), and directional in the diffusion-map embedding (0.072), confirming the convergence is geometry, not an artifact of the linear PCA basis (Table S3). The two-cell **core** alone is too small for the compactness statistic (a single pairwise distance; percentile 0.07–0.24, not significant) — identity attaches to the core (which two cells), invariance to the region (whether the neighborhood is tight). The two carriers are kept distinct.

**Table S3. Coordinate-invariance compactness, region versus bare core, in three bases** (`tableS3_invariance.csv`).

| Set | Coordinates | Percentile | z |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| neighborhood (4) | PCA (9-D) | **0.005** | −2.09 |
| neighborhood (4) | varimax (6-D) | **0.001** | −2.38 |
| neighborhood (4) | diffusion (8-D) | 0.072 | −0.83 |
| core (2) | PCA (9-D) | 0.113 (ns) | −1.13 |
| core (2) | varimax (6-D) | 0.073 (ns) | −1.32 |
| core (2) | diffusion (8-D) | 0.243 (ns) | −0.53 |

The diffusion embedding is no tighter than PCA, consistent with the manifold being only weakly curved (PCA adequate). Compactness in the pseudobulk calibration is diffuse (percentile ~0.46); the tightness is specific to the single_cell_scaled calibration used for tumor projection, as expected from the calibration's construction. This is not calibration-shopping: Note S-H shows the pseudobulk alternative degenerates independently, on normal single cells with no cancer involved, collapsing 99.6% of them onto erythroid targets — so the diffuseness here is a property of the wrong calibration for single-cell data, not evidence that the tightness was selected for.

## Note S5 — Metabolic-enzyme stratification (secondary)

On the normal side, convergent-region cells are glycolytic-oxidative-active and distinct from NAMPT-pathway inflammatory cells; on the cancer side the corresponding stratification is null. Reported as a secondary observation only; it does not bear on the convergence result.

**Terminology and boundaries (glossary).** *Regulatory module* and *perceptual module* denote the same object; the main text uses "regulatory module" throughout. Region boundaries, used consistently: the **two-cell core** = cytotrophoblast + megakaryocyte; the **three-cell core** = those two plus the migrating cytotrophoblast (used in the control notes; see Note S-K); the **four-cell majority-enriched neighbourhood** and the **14-cell broader region** are successively wider boundaries used only in the boundary-independence check (Table S2), which shows the enrichment survives the choice. *The vocabulary, fixed.* The paper uses several near-synonyms and they are not interchangeable, so each is pinned here. **The region** (equivalently *convergent region*, *convergent state*) is whichever of the four boundaries above is under discussion, and is always qualified in context; it is the object of the claim this paper defends. **The core** without a number means the two-cell core. **The target** is the cell a ranking puts nearest the region — a property of the ranking, not an identification of the state (§3). **The anchor** is the target under *this* module catalog and at the readiness level of measurement, i.e. the cytotrophoblast; independently reconstructed catalogs reproduce the region and anchor it elsewhere (Note S-O), and output-level scoring does the same (Note S-T), so "anchor" never carries a claim beyond this catalog. **Occupancy** is the share of cells whose top-1 nearest normal neighbour is a member of the boundary named; **depth** is the per-cancer occupancy; **recurrence** is the number of cancers in which a cell is among the five most frequent nearest neighbours. Where a sentence needs the distinction between region and cell and does not make it, the region is meant.

**Note S-A. Robustness of the convergence to the assignment rule and to transcriptional magnitude (`scripts/E3_referee_controls.py`).** Re-scoring the 25 cancers (83,819 malignant cells) and re-projecting: the winner-take-all top-1 core fraction (27%) is matched by a soft cosine-softmax assignment (21% core mass) and by top-k membership (core in the top 3 of 152 for 57% of cells, top 5 for 75%; median nearest-core rank 3), so the convergence is not an artifact of hard assignment. It is not the low-complexity/magnitude artifact that inflated the erythrocyte: core-assigned cells carry higher transcriptional complexity than non-core (median 2,629 vs 2,360 detected genes), and cells assigned to a centroid do not track that centroid's projection norm (r = +0.14). Applying the same magnitude-removing transform to BOTH reference and cancer (within-cell centering, or per-cell rank transform) reduces the core convergence from 27% to 7–11% (still 4–5× over the ~2% chance baseline): the effect is largely a signal of overall regulatory output (the terminal-high-output biology) with a smaller, real module-shape residual.



**Note S-B. Calibration dependence (`scripts/E4_calibration.py`).** Scoring the 25 cancers once and projecting under each reference calibration: under the single-cell-scaled calibration the core fraction is 17% and the 25 malignant centroids are tighter than every one of 2,000 random 25-cell draws; under a pseudobulk (population-averaged) calibration the core fraction falls to 7% and the projection degenerates, collapsing tumor cells toward low-complexity non-core targets (mean pairwise centroid distance 0.001). Convergence onto the core is present under both calibrations but is properly resolved only under the single-cell calibration — the appropriate one for single-cell tumor data, and the choice we adopt on unit-matching grounds (not because it maximizes the effect).



**Note S-C. The address is specific, not merely a high-output signal (`scripts/E5_specificity.py`, 83,819 malignant cells, 25 cancers).** Four independent tests separate *which cell is named* from *overall transcriptional level*. (i) *Shape-only ranking.* Applying the magnitude-removing rank transform to both reference and cancer and re-ranking the cross-cancer recurrence, the cytotrophoblast remains the single most recurrent normal target (14/25 cancers) and the syncytiotrophoblast — a second trophoblast state — rises to second (9/25); the megakaryocyte falls from tied-first to sixth. Magnitude removal therefore *sharpens* the address onto the trophoblast lineage (the named anchor) and reveals that the residual magnitude dependence lives in the megakaryocyte arm, not in the cytotrophoblast. (ii) *High-output specificity.* The professional high-output secretory normals that a pure-magnitude account predicts should also attract convergence do not: of all cross-cancer top-1 votes, hepatocytes receive 0.11%, plasma cells 0.15%, parietal cells 0.69%, prostatic glandular 0.03%, proximal-tubule 0.00% — against 11.1% (cytotrophoblast) and 12.8% (megakaryocyte) for the core. The only high-baseline normals that draw appreciable convergence, pancreatic acinar (7.5%) and gastric chief (7.3%), are the resident secretory cells of two cancers in the panel (PDAC, gastric) — tissue-of-origin return rather than cross-lineage convergence. (iii) *Library-size control.* Core assignment is essentially uncorrelated with sequencing depth: r(core, log10 total counts) = +0.03, r(core, detected genes) = +0.04. (iv) *Orthogonal markers.* Scored on trophoblast/EVT genes lying entirely outside the 206 module genes, core-assigned malignant cells express the extravillous-trophoblast invasion program more highly than non-core (MMP2 0.140 vs 0.082; MMP9 0.098 vs 0.042; ITGA5 0.090 vs 0.064), though the canonical identity markers (HLA-G, CGA/CGB, KRT7, TFAP2C) are not specifically enriched — a modest, invasion-flavored independent corroboration of trophoblast-ness, not a full identity match.



**Note S-D. Within-patient deepening under a mixed-effects model.** The pooled sign test (77/86 patients deeper) treats patients as exchangeable across the four low-baseline datasets, which risks a Simpson's-paradox confound. A linear mixed-effects model of the per-patient malignant-minus-normal core difference with a dataset random intercept estimates a fixed deepening of +13.0 percentage points (SE 1.87; z = 6.95; one-sided *P* = 1.9 × 10⁻¹²), with all four datasets deepening in the mean (colorectal-Pelka 43/46, colorectal-HTA 23/25, kidney 6/7, liver 5/8). The effect thus survives crediting the between-dataset clustering; we note the coarseness of a four-group random-effect estimate (two of the four cohorts colorectal), so the low-baseline conclusion is carried by colorectal with same-signed support from kidney and liver.



**Note S-F. The deepening survives within-pair complexity matching (`scripts/E15_deepening_complexity_matched.py`).** Malignant epithelium carries more detected genes than matched-normal (colorectal median 3,852 vs 2,272), and core assignment is weakly complexity-dependent, so a within-pair complexity offset could in principle manufacture deepening. Resampling the malignant cells of each patient to the *matched-normal* detected-gene distribution (nearest-gene-count matching, 20 draws) and recomputing the core fraction: the deepening falls only from +15.5 to +12.9 percentage points (43/46 patients, paired *P* = 10⁻⁸), retaining ~80% of the raw effect. The paired complexity offset therefore accounts for at most a fifth of the deepening.



**Note S-G. The contraction is mostly magnitude and averaging, with a real shape residue (`scripts/E14_contraction_matched_null.py`).** The raw 25-cancer clustering (mean pairwise cosine distance ~0.166, vs ~0.99 among the near-orthogonal origins) is largely a matched-averaging and shared-magnitude effect: 25 equal-sized coherent normal micro-populations, formed by the same averaging over a seven-tissue normal pool, cluster nearly as tightly (null mean 0.129, 95% interval [0.074, 0.213]), and the malignant clustering is flat across subsample size (0.174 at 25 cells/cancer, 0.166 at 4,000), so it is a stable biological quantity rather than the shrinkage of large means. Removing the overall-magnitude component isolates a residue that we report as a specification curve rather than a single number, because the comparison is sensitive to how the magnitude-removed scores are rescaled before projection. (i) In the **raw module space**, with no standardization or projection at all, the malignant centroids cluster at 0.326 against a null mean of 0.493 — below every one of 200 draws (and in the same space the raw metric is 0.168 vs 0.287, likewise below every draw). (ii) Under the **reference standardization used throughout the paper**, the shape-only clustering is 0.026 against a null of 0.034 (the observed value falls below 292 of 300 matched-averaging draws; permutation *P* = (8+1)/(300+1) = 0.030). (iii) Under a third standardization — dividing by the standard deviations of the *magnitude-removed* reference, which up-weights modules whose pattern barely varies across the 154 normal types and so amplifies low-variance axes — the advantage vanishes (0.400 vs 0.382, 66th percentile). Two of three specifications, including the simplest and assumption-free one, support a real shape convergence; one does not. We report all three and no longer headline the magnitude-inflated 6.3-fold figure. (The coherent-micro-population null is if anything biased tight, making the surviving results conservative; a cell-type-level null is the natural refinement.)



**Note S-H. Cancer-blind justification of the single-cell calibration (`scripts/E16_calibration_cancerblind.py`).** To show the single-cell-scaled calibration is chosen on principled, not result-maximizing, grounds, we test it on 16,312 independent *normal* single cells (skin, kidney, breast; author cell types; no cancer). Projected and assigned to their nearest normal reference type: the **pseudobulk** calibration degenerates — it collapses these normal single cells onto only 2 of 152 targets (effective assignment diversity 1.0), 99.6% of them forced onto low-complexity erythroid cells — the same degeneration it produces on tumor cells (Note S-B). The **single-cell-scaled** calibration keeps the assignments diverse and complexity-appropriate (85 targets, diversity 14.6). Pseudobulk is thus wrong for single-cell data of any kind, normal or malignant; the single-cell calibration is required by the data type and selected with no cancer input. (A fine-cell-type silhouette metric did not distinguish the calibrations; we report the degeneration test, not the metric that would have favored our choice.)



**Note S-I. Malignant-cell purity and the direction of its bias.** The lung dilution artifact (Fig. S11-II) shows automated malignant selection can be wrong in a specific cohort, so we state the purity basis of each within-patient cohort and the direction of any residual bias. The cohorts carrying the deepening use author-annotated malignant compartments — Pelka `ClusterMidway = EpiT` (tumor epithelial clusters) and lung Kim-2020 tumor subtypes tS1/tS2/tS3 — which are high-purity; the gastric arm uses an EPCAM+/CD45− gate on the cancer sample with no malignant annotation, so it may retain some normal-adjacent epithelium. Crucially the bias is signed: normal-adjacent contamination pulls a tumor arm toward its normal baseline and therefore *understates* deepening (the lung case: a 9%-malignant arm read ~13% ≈ normal, versus ~29% for the clean malignant cells; `E13`). Marker-based selection (germ-cell/sarcoma) biases convergence the other way and is bounded by the drop-4 robustness. We do not, however, claim that contamination makes the reported numbers a lower bound: we tested that argument directly and it was not borne out (below). We tested the contamination argument directly on the two cohorts whose labels allow it — restricting HCC to hepatocytes from tumour sites and BRCA to annotated cancer-epithelial cells — and it is *not* borne out: cleaning lowered rather than raised those cohorts (HCC 24.3% → 18.6%, BRCA 31.0% → 26.7%) and left the panel essentially unchanged (core 27.1% → 26.6% raw, 17.3% → 17.1% shape-only; clustering 0.166 → 0.166). Contamination therefore does not appear to be masking convergence, and we report the direction-of-bias argument as untested-and-unsupported rather than as a defence. A CNV-inference malignant floor across all 25 cohorts is deferred to production, as the convergence is now presented as the modest, magnitude-carried companion (Note S-G). Separately, on the deepening-vs-baseline relationship: across five cohorts the additive deepening does not depend on baseline (Pearson *r* = +0.4, *P* = 0.5; mean ~+10 points), the fold-change inverse being a small-denominator artifact — so we make no baseline-gating claim (Fig. S11-IIIB).



**Note S-J. What moves the state, and what does not (perturbation and immune controls).** *Single genes do not.* Across the genome-wide Perturb-seq resource (Replogle et al. 2022; 9,675 CRISPRi knockdowns in K562 with 75,328 non-targeting controls), knocking down a target-defining brake does not move cells toward the core: the correlation between a gene's brake score and its knockdown's core shift is +0.07 (*P* = 0.4), +0.13 after controlling for the generic brightening that essential-gene knockdown causes (*P* = 0.1). A three-level decomposition shows where the signal stops (`scripts/E21_why_null_diagnostic.py`): CRISPRi works (target mRNA down, median −0.43 SD), the containing module responds strongly (LDHA→HIF −1.84 SD, HSP90AA1→HSF1 −1.65; and, as a positive control that the modules read pathway biology rather than their own transcripts, KEAP1 knockdown *raises* the NRF2 module by +0.96 SD), but among the 23 strong module hits the nine-dimensional position moves only a fifth as much and in no consistent direction (13 of 23 toward the core; *r* = −0.10 between module movement and coordinate movement). We read this as a many-module consensus buffered against single-gene perturbation — consistent with a state defined by a dozen simultaneously suppressed brakes, and with the large movement seen under whole-network pressure (Fig. S14). We label that reading for what it is: a post-hoc account of a failed causal prediction, not a tested one. Two limits bound it. The screen is K562, a single erythroleukemia line and an unfavourable substrate for a state anchored on trophoblast and epithelial biology; we do not report its unperturbed position in the coordinate, so we cannot exclude that the cells had little room to move toward the core in the first place — that baseline should accompany any future use of this screen. And no combinatorial knockdown was run, so the “coordinated pressure” that the account requires is untested rather than supported. *Immune privilege is not inherited.* Of the three properties that motivate the cytotrophoblast — invasion, proliferation, immune evasion — the first is corroborated by orthogonal markers (Note S-C) but the third is not: across 62 colorectal tumors the malignant core fraction is unrelated to immune infiltration (Spearman ρ = +0.13 raw and +0.04 shape-only for T/NK cells, *P* = 0.3 and 0.8; myeloid +0.16 and +0.03), and stratifying by mismatch-repair status — the dominant confounder — leaves it unchanged (MMRd ρ = +0.06, n = 34; MMRp ρ = +0.08, n = 28). Sixty-two tumors exclude a moderate association. This is what the coordinate predicts: Type I interferon is the one target-defining brake cancer does not reproduce, so cancer reaches the address without its immune privilege. Cell-fraction infiltration from dissociated tissue is noisy and infiltration is not exclusion; spatial and functional tests remain open. `scripts/E20_gwps_causal.py`, `scripts/E27_immune_exclusion.py`.



**Note S-K. Definitions, denominators and how the reported percentages relate (reader key).** Three choices govern every percentage in this paper and we fix them here. (i) *Gallery denominator.* The reference holds 154 normal cell types; the two post-cellular bodies (erythrocyte, platelet) are quarantined as a documented low-complexity artifact, leaving **152 candidates** for every assignment. The quarantine is applied *before* the nearest-neighbour step, not after it: the two cells are removed from the candidate pool and the cells that would have gone to them are reassigned to their best surviving neighbour, rather than being dropped from the denominator. Two implementations of this existed in earlier drafts and gave different top-4 boundaries; `scripts/E41_regen_ranking.py` now regenerates the ranking, the boundary table and the per-cancer matrix from one assignment, and Table S2, Table 1, Fig. 3B, 3C, 3E and 3F are all derived from that single artifact. Ranks are quoted out of 154 only where the quarantined cells are deliberately shown. (ii) *Which cells define the measured state, and where each definition is used.* Two definitions appear and we separate them explicitly, because they belong to different sections. The **ranking statistics of §3** — cross-cancer occupancy and its enrichment — are quoted for the single most recurrent cell (cytotrophoblast alone, chance 1/152 = 0.66%) and for the **two-cell core** (cytotrophoblast + megakaryocyte, chance 2/152 = 1.3%). The **within-patient occupancy of §5, which is the paper’s primary assay, uses a three-cell definition** that additionally includes the migrating cytotrophoblast, the trophoblast state adjacent to the first (chance 3/152 = 2.0%); every number in §5 and every control quoting a “~2% chance baseline” is on that definition (`scripts/pelka_perpatient.py`, `E10`, `E12`, `E17`, `E36`). An earlier version of this note stated that the main text used the two-cell definition throughout, which was wrong for §5. Splitting the colorectal effect by member shows why the distinction matters: the trophoblast states carry +16.6 points of the increase and the megakaryocyte −0.8. (iii) *Metric.* The primary statistic is the hard top-1 assignment under the single-cell-scaled calibration. The values a reader will meet are therefore: **23.9%** (top-2 core, main text), **27%** (three-cell core, top-1, the 83,819-cell control panel) and **17%** (three-cell core with the eigenspace rebuilt per calibration, Note S-B; that run has no shape-only variant, so 17% and 17.3% below are *not* the same number). Soft assignment gives 21% core mass for the same cells, and Note S-C reports the two core cells separately (11.1% + 12.8% = 23.9% of cross-cancer votes) rather than as one fraction. These are the same phenomenon under different definitions, not conflicting measurements; the numbers the abstract leads with are the raw ones, and each shape-only value is labelled as such where it appears. (iv) *Shape-only, defined.* “Shape-only” means: subtract from every cell (reference and malignant alike) its own mean across the 44 module scores, `X - X.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)`, **before** the reference standardization, and recompute the reference mean and SD on the centred reference; projection, normalization and top-1 assignment are otherwise unchanged. This is the convention used in the main text and throughout the figures, and under it the three-cell core falls from 27.1% to **17.3%** (`scripts/E23_purity_fixed_convergence.py`). Note S-A quotes **7–11%** for what reads as the same operation: it is not. There the centring is applied *after* z-standardization, against the unchanged reference statistics (`scripts/E3_referee_controls.py`), which removes more than the magnitude component; on the same 83,819 cells the two orders give 7.4% and 10.7% against 17.3%. Both are honest shape-only variants and both leave the core four- to fivefold over chance, but they are different transforms and we do not average or interchange them. *(v) The two enrichment panels.* The three boundary enrichments are quoted twice in this paper from two different panels, and the difference is the panel, not the result. **Table 1 and Fig. 3F give 18.2-fold, 15.9-fold and 8.4-fold** on the 83,819-cell, 25-cohort panel that every main-text statistic uses. **Note S-Q(i) gives 16.0-fold, 11.5-fold and 6.6-fold**, and 15.8, 12.5 and 6.0 after dropping the complexity-failing members, on the 312,220-cell, 17-cohort pool — that control re-scores every tracked target across the full stored cohorts rather than the capped panel, which is why its baseline differs. Neither supersedes the other: the first is the panel, the second is the control run on the pool where the control can be run. A third 17-cohort figure appears in Note S-V (17.0, 16.3 and 8.7) and is a **different** seventeen — the cohorts with a genuine malignant annotation, on the capped panel — so the two "17 cohorts" in this paper are not the same set and are never compared with each other.



**Note S-L. Five further methodological controls.** *(i) Map validity, with the right null.* A cell's nearest neighbour in the nine-dimensional space shares its biological family in **62% of the 152 candidate cell types (95% CI 55–70%)**. The relevant null is not 1/K over families (which would be 7% and is too permissive of the claim) but a family-size-preserving one: permuting the family labels 10,000 times over the same neighbour graph gives a null mean of **14%** (maximum 28%) and a permutation **P = 1 × 10⁻⁴**, which is the floor 10,000 draws can reach rather than a measured value. The 15% previously quoted as "chance" corresponds to this size-preserving rate, not to 1/K. *(ii) Modules share a few genes.* Twelve genes appear in more than one module (ADIPOQ, CALM1, CAMK2A, CCND1, DUSP1, FKBP5, FOXO1, GSK3B, PGR, SGK1, STAT1, XBP1), touching 17 of the 44 modules; 219 gene slots reduce to 206 unique genes. Of the six module pairs with |r| > 0.7, **two are partly definitional** — UPR-ATF6 with UPR-IRE1 (share XBP1) and JAK-STAT with Type I IFN (share STAT1) — while four (ERK/MAPK–NF-κB, UPR-PERK–p53, NF-κB–UPR-PERK, BMP–Hippo) share no genes and are biological. We therefore no longer describe all six as "biologically expected". Recomputing the two definitional correlations on disjoint gene sets requires gene-level reference scores and is listed as a production item. *(iii) Dimensionality.* Three of the nine retained components are the least determined under bootstrap (PC7–PC9, 0.53–0.71 over 500 draws), so we report the result at six as well as nine dimensions: the core fraction is 18.9% (6-D) versus 27.1% (9-D) and the contraction 0.131 versus 0.166 — and in six dimensions the **cytotrophoblast becomes the single top recurrent target**, ahead of the megakaryocyte. The magnitude depends on dimensionality; the anchor does not. *(iv) Leave-one-cancer-out.* Dropping each of the 25 cohorts in turn — not only the four marker-labelled ones — the core fraction stays within **25.4–28.4%** (full panel 27.1%) and the cytotrophoblast's recurrence rank is **2 in every one of the 25 panels** (top-3 in 25/25); the most influential single cohort is B-ALL. No cohort drives the result. *(v) Specification multiplicity.* The shape-only convergence is supported by two of three scalings — in raw module space it falls below every one of 200 null draws, under the paper's reference scaling the permutation P is 0.030, and under the magnitude-removed-reference scaling there is no effect. A Bonferroni correction across the three specifications leaves the raw-module-space result significant and renders the reference-scaled one marginal (P = 0.09); we report the curve rather than a single corrected number. `scripts/E34_methodology_controls.py`.



**Note S-M. Supporting detail for six claims made in the Results.** (i) *Lung correction, full account.* On the reference lung data themselves the cleanly identified malignant cells sit about 18 points above the normal epithelium with which they had been averaged; the diluted arm was ~9% annotated-malignant (Fig. S11-II; scripts/E12_highbaseline_lung.py, scripts/E13_lung_dilution_proof.py). (ii) *Prostate.* A fifteen-patient paired atlas (CELLxGENE) shows no within-patient increase (7/15) at a normal baseline of ~15%; an independent paired cohort (Song et al. 2022, GSE176031, four patients) puts the benign baseline at 2.1% and the tumour arm at 3.8% (paired P = 0.38). The two cohorts disagree about the baseline and neither shows an increase, so prostate is reported as an open negative. (iii) *The fold-change impression.* Across five tissues the added occupancy is flat against baseline on the additive scale (Pearson r = +0.4, P = 0.5) and not significant on a bounded logit scale; the appearance of more room where the baseline is lower comes from dividing by colorectal's 4.7% denominator. (iv) *Fibrotic lung, per cell type.* Within IPF epithelium the shape-only core fraction is highest in transitional AT2 (18.4%) and proliferating epithelium (16.2%) and lowest in ciliated cells (5.2%), all below the malignant lung level of 30.3% (Fig. S12). (v) *Stage independence.* The occupancy increase is already saturated at the earliest stages sampled and is independent of tumour grade and stage in the best-powered cohort. (vi) *Germline alternative.* The germline matches the core on PC1 (cosine 1.00) but diverges across the remaining eight components; by the full recurrence-and-depth ranking the nearest germline cells fall well below the core, stable across 2,000 bootstrap resamples of the 25 cancers.

**Note S-N. The module panel, evaluated without cancer data.** Everything in this note is computed on the 154 normal cell types; no cancer data enter. *(i) Coherence — the modules are units.* For each module we take the mean pairwise Pearson correlation among its genes across the 154 normal cell types. Median within-module coherence is +0.154 for the 44 modules (median 5 genes each), against +0.014 for 200 random gene sets matched on size and drawn from expressed genes (Mann-Whitney P = 3.2e-15), and against +0.062 for KEGG signalling maps (median 121 genes), +0.058 for the largest Reactome pathways (153), +0.070 for Hallmark (179) and +0.051 for PROGENy (99); the modules beat every collection at P = 1e-4 to 6e-9 while carrying twenty- to thirty-fold fewer genes. *(ii) Readability — why a transcriptional output is required.* Grouping every annotated gene of every module by the role it plays and taking its standard deviation across the 154 normal cell types, the induced arm (feedback elements and TF targets, n = 387) has median SD 0.287 against 0.222 for the structural arm (sensors and cascade components, n = 704), P = 2.8e-05. A pathway is readable from RNA through what it induces, not through the abundance of its receptors and kinases. *(iii) Catalog size.* Random subsets of k of the 44 modules, with the eigenspace rebuilt from the normal cells alone, recover nearest-neighbour biological-family concordance of 38.7% (k=5), 54.5% (15), 61.2% (30), 63.0% (40) and 65.8% (44), and agree with the sampled-catalog cell-to-cell geometry at r = 0.53, 0.78, 0.92, 0.976 and 1.0 — the value at k = 44 being a comparison with itself, not an independent agreement. Ninety-five per cent of the full value is reached at k = 35 on both measures and both are still rising at 44. One caveat on the endpoint: this curve rebuilds the eigenspace from each sampled subset, so its k = 44 value (65.8%, 100 of 152) is not the 62% (95 of 152) quoted in the main text and in Fig. 1C, which is measured in the shipped, fixed nine-dimensional reference (`reference_v03`). The two spaces are near-identical but not identical (cell-to-cell geometry r = 0.964), and the 3.3-point gap is the size of that difference, not a disagreement about the statistic. The catalog therefore sits at the shoulder of saturation. This bounds whether 44 is sufficient; it cannot say whether a larger catalog built on different criteria would do better. *(iv) What we could NOT establish — the negative-feedback criterion.* We tried twice to support criterion (iii) empirically and report both failures. Against the Cancer Gene Census (v103, 740 genes), the genes occupying the feedback position of a module are NOT enriched for cancer-driver status relative to the other genes of the same modules: 17/114 (14.9%) versus 128/638 (20.1%), OR = 0.70, P = 0.25, and significantly in the opposite direction against tighter backgrounds (core genes only, OR = 0.33, P = 0.004). And in (ii) above the feedback genes alone are only marginally more variable than cascade genes (0.242 versus 0.222, P = 0.16) — the induced-arm effect is carried by TF targets. We therefore present negative feedback as the panel's organizing design principle and not as an empirically validated selection rule. `scripts/E37_feedback_cgc_enrichment.py`, `perceptome_validation/scripts/E38_cancerfree_module_justification.py`, `perceptome_validation/scripts/E39_saturation.py`.



**Note S-O. How much of the result depends on this particular catalog, and on which normal cells are compared.** *(i) Independent reconstruction of the catalog.* Three curations were produced by independent large-language-model agents from the four operational criteria and the size target alone, with the incumbent catalog, this paper, and the words trophoblast and megakaryocyte withheld; each shares about half of the incumbent's 206 genes (Jaccard 0.28-0.31) while agreeing with one another far more closely (0.62-0.69). What reproduces is the REGION: the top-ten nearest targets overlap the incumbent's by 5-7 of 10, and five cell types - gastric progenitor, gastric chief, pancreatic acinar, oesophageal apical and megakaryocyte - are in the top ten of all four catalogs. What does not reproduce is the ANCHOR: all three curations place the cytotrophoblast 26th-35th of 152 by cosine to the malignant attractor (0.507-0.639 against 0.955-0.963 for their own winner, the gastric progenitor), and 0 of 3 put it in the top five. The cosine band is flat only at the very top (rank 1 to rank 10 is 0.96 to 0.84) and has fallen to 0.51-0.62 by rank 30, so this is a real separation and not an artifact of ordinal ranking. Between-catalog variance, which the framework previously did not have: core occupancy 12.0 ± 4.3% against 24.7% for the incumbent — the three-cell core on the rebuilt eigenspace this comparison uses, which is the right comparator here and is not the 23.9% two-cell main-text figure (Note S-K). Caveat stated plainly: the three curators are language-model agents of one model family and share its priors (Use of generative AI), which explains their agreement with each other but not their disagreement with the incumbent. *(ii) Established pathway collections.* KEGG (46 signalling maps), Reactome (44 largest pathways), Hallmark (50) and PROGENy (14), each scored through the identical pipeline, reproduce the within-patient occupancy increase at a comparable size (KEGG +15.0 points shape-only, 44/46 patients; PROGENy +12.9; Hallmark +19.2 raw, 46/46; Reactome, the weakest, +6.5 raw and +2.5 shape-only, 33/46, *P* = 0.014; incumbent +9.0 shape-only and +13.9 raw), and under every one of them the largest within-patient shift of colorectal epithelium is toward a trophoblast state. All six numbers in that comparison, the incumbent's included, come from one harness run under a single common setting — pseudobulk loadings with the eigenspace rebuilt per panel — chosen so the collections are scored identically. That is why the incumbent reads +9.0/+13.9 here and +10.5/+15.5 in the main text and Note S-F, where it is scored under the single-cell-matched calibration the tumour data require: same 46 patients, same cells, different calibration (under which raw and shape-only nearly exchange places, 8.81/10.98). The comparison between collections is therefore valid; only the incumbent's absolute value differs from the body of the paper (`perceptome_validation/registry/provenance_notes.md`). None of them reproduces the cross-lineage cytotrophoblast target: their core fractions are 0.05-0.29 times the incumbent's and they place the cytotrophoblast 12th-34th, recurrent in 0-2 of the 25 cancers. The occupancy increase is therefore catalog-independent; the cross-lineage generality of the target is not. *(iii) Which normal cells the comparison uses.* Colorectal cancer arises from crypt-base stem and transit-amplifying cells, not from the tissue at large, so the matched-normal arm must be the right subtype. Scoring whole transcriptomes on 2,000 highly variable genes rather than on the modules, the malignant epithelium of 57 of 62 patients is nearest the proliferating stem/TA population (cE03), and each tumour subtype is nearest its own normal counterpart (Tumor cE03 to cE03, r = 0.74). Restricting the normal arm accordingly: malignant versus normal stem/TA, higher in 35 of 39 patients (+14.8 points); subtype against the same subtype, +14.6 (cE01) and +15.4 (cE03). Normal crypt-base cells occupy the state at 7-9% against 0.1-1% for differentiated enterocytes, so the unrestricted comparison overstates the effect (+19.1 points) and the crypt-base comparison is reported as primary. *(iv) Decoy modules, and what the two robustness percentages are really comparing.* Adding five modules of random genes (44 to 49) requires scoring new modules gene by gene, which the reduced 23-cancer harness does and the full pipeline of Fig. S7-II does not, so no full-fidelity decoy arm exists. Within the reduced harness, where both arms are measured the same way, the decoy arm is not the harsher perturbation: the cytotrophoblast holds the top three by cross-cancer recurrence in 42 of 100 decoy draws against 96 of 200 draws that instead drop five real modules (48%; Fisher *P* = 0.39, rank distributions Mann-Whitney *P* = 0.49), and on the quantitative endpoints the decoy arm is marginally the better of the two (median core fraction 19.0% against 17.7%, enrichment 9.7x against 9.1x). The 42% should therefore not be read against the 84% of Fig. S7-II: those are two rankings of the same perturbation class, not two perturbations. The cytotrophoblast is rank 1 by cosine to the malignant attractor and rank 3 by cross-cancer recurrence when nothing is perturbed, so “stays in the top three” is nearly free in the first ranking and close to a coin-flip in the second: the same 200 five-module drops give 84% by attractor rank and 44% by recurrence rank. `scripts/E2d_module_robustness.py`, `scripts/E2e_module_robustness_fullfid.py`. *(v) Single genes that move the anchor.* Rebuilding the catalog 206 times, once per gene with that gene removed, leaves the cytotrophoblast at rank 1–2 in 202 of the 206 rebuilds (core occupancy 15.4–30.7%). **Note S-T. The same modules scored by regulatory *output* instead of component *abundance*: the convergence grows, the address does not survive (pre-registered test T7; `perceptome_validation/scripts/T7_activity_scoring.py`).** A module score in this paper is the mean abundance of the module's component transcripts. That measures how *equipped* a cell is for a pathway, not how active the pathway is: activation is largely post-translational and reaches RNA only through induced targets. The distinction is structural rather than a technicality, and a framework named for perception that scores the parts list owes the reader a test at the other level. We pre-registered one, with its three endpoints, its thresholds and a one-sided directional prediction fixed before the data were read.

*Method.* All 44 modules were re-scored by regulatory output. Thirty-four modules whose output is a transcription factor were scored as regulon activity over that factor's DoRothEA A/B regulon (signed weights, 6–755 targets; the union where a module names more than one factor — for example HIF through HIF1A + EPAS1, JAK-STAT through STAT3 + STAT1). ERK/MAPK and PI3K/PTEN, which name no transcription-factor output, were scored by their PROGENy footprints (99 and 100 genes). The remaining eight — AMPK, Autophagy, MR, mTOR, NPAS4, PXR/CAR, TR and UPR-IRE1 — have no usable regulon and were scored by the mean expression of the catalog's own documented target genes, which is the weakest arm of the test and is stated as such. VIPER is the pre-registered primary estimator; ULM is reported as the linear alternative. The 154-type reference and all 83,819 malignant cells were re-scored identically, cohort by cohort on that cohort's present genes; the eigenspace was then rebuilt from the activity-scored 154 × 44 matrix (Kaiser rule as everywhere else) and the whole pipeline re-run under both calibrations, with and without magnitude removal. The erythrocyte/platelet quarantine of Note S-K applies throughout, so the candidate pool is 152 and the three-cell chance baseline is 2.0%. The per-module definitions are frozen in `perceptome_validation/registry/T7_activity_definitions.json`.

*Verdict: FAIL, under both estimators.* Two of the three pre-registered conditions hold, with room to spare. Core occupancy at the output level is 33.3% (VIPER) and 32.8% (ULM) against a floor of 11.7% and against 24.7% for readiness scoring in the same harness — the cross-lineage convergence is *stronger*, not weaker, when modules are read through what they induce. The magnitude-removed within-patient deepening is +19.5 points (VIPER, 41 of 46) and +8.3 (ULM, 35 of 46) against a threshold of 5 points and the same sign, and against +4.9 for readiness scoring in this harness — three to four times larger. The test fails on the third condition, and decisively under the primary estimator: the **cytotrophoblast falls to rank 17 of 152 and is a top target in 0 of 25 cancers** (ULM: rank 4, 13 of 25), and the output-level target is the **megakaryocyte in 25 of 25 cancers** (ULM: the oocyte in 21 of 25). The cytotrophoblast is outside the top five in all eight activity specifications. Read straight: measured by regulatory output rather than by parts list, cross-lineage convergence onto one region is stronger and the within-patient deepening is stronger in shape-only terms — and the cell converged upon is not the cytotrophoblast. The pre-registration states what this means and we adopt its words: the convergence is about *equipment* for a state rather than occupancy of it, which is a materially different claim. The identity reported in §3 is therefore scoped twice over — to this catalog (Note S-O) and to readiness-level measurement.

*One raw endpoint collapses, and we do not fold it into the others.* Under VIPER, the within-patient deepening without magnitude removal is only +1.5 points, higher in 26 of 46 patients, *P* = 0.20 — a null, where the same arm reads +13.9 under readiness scoring and +14.5 under ULM. At the output level the Pelka deepening is carried entirely by module shape under one estimator and by both components under the other. The deepening claim of §5 is therefore robust to magnitude removal at the readiness level (where it is primary) but not estimator-independent at the output level.

*The directional prediction was confirmed.* Pre-registered one-sided: the shape-only fraction of E1 should rise relative to raw under activity scoring. It rises from 0.51 under readiness to 0.98 (VIPER) and 0.99 (ULM) — at the output level the raw and shape-only convergences coincide. Magnitude-carried dilution therefore does explain the gap between the two at the readiness level, which means the paper's "largely a shared high-output signal" caveat (Note S-A, Discussion) is a property of readiness-level measurement and not of the phenomenon.

*A caveat that cuts both ways, stated rather than used.* The paper's own complexity control (Fig. S5-II, Fig. S7) shows megakaryocyte assignment rising as detected-gene count falls, and VIPER's rank-based enrichment is sensitive to how many of a regulon's targets are detected; part of the 25-of-25 megakaryocyte may be that same low-complexity artifact appearing at a new measurement level. That is a reason to hold the output-level *target* loosely — it is not a reason to retain the cytotrophoblast, which is absent from the top five in every activity specification tested, under both estimators and both calibrations.

*An independent reproduction.* The *in vitro* series of Note S-R were analysed under the same two estimators as a pre-registered amendment. In the ten-donor paired series (GSE240057) the activity-space attractor's nearest normal cells are the megakaryocyte, the oocyte and spermatids, with the cytotrophoblast absent from the VIPER top eight — the T7 result reproduced in a dataset that contributed nothing to it.

*Which scoring the framework adopts, as the pre-registration requires us to state.* Readiness-level scoring remains primary and is what every number in this paper is computed under: it is the level the catalog was built for, the level at which the four operational criteria were applied, and the level at which the module-level and gene-level orthogonal signatures of §4 were established. Activity-level scoring is adopted as a required robustness test and reported at every endpoint rather than as an alternative primary, for two stated reasons: eight of the 44 modules have no regulon and fall back to mean target expression, so the two levels are not measured with equal quality across the catalog; and the complexity sensitivity above is unresolved at the output level. What changes is the scope of the claims, not the numbers: the increase in occupancy and the cross-lineage convergence are reported as measurement-level-robust, and the identity of the convergent cell is reported as specific to readiness-level measurement, in §3, §4 and the Discussion.

*Provenance of the readiness rows.* They are this test's own internal baseline and are not the main text's figures. T7 rebuilds the eigenspace from the matrix it scores, so under magnitude removal the loadings themselves are rebuilt from the magnitude-removed reference (which is why *K* rises from 9 to 12 in the second row). The main text's shape-only control (+10.5 points; `scripts/E17_deepening_shapeonly.py`, Note S-A) and the panel comparison of Note S-O(ii) (+9.0) instead hold the loadings fixed and remove magnitude at the projection step. The raw rows agree to the decimal (+13.9 here and in Note S-O(ii)); the shape-only rows differ because of that rebuilt-versus-fixed eigenspace, not because of a disagreement about the effect. Every comparison drawn inside this note is between rows of this table, scored in one harness under one setting, which is the only purpose the readiness rows serve. The two-cell readiness figure here, 21.9%, likewise is not the main text's 23.9% for the same reason (Note S-K, `perceptome_validation/registry/provenance_notes.md`).

**Note S-V. The naming, recomputed without the cohorts whose malignant compartment is a cell-type label (`scripts/E45_genuine_label_subset.py`).** Note S-U reports that in **eight of the fourteen** Census cohorts “malignant” is not a malignancy annotation but a normal lineage label (ovarian serous, prostate, neuroblastoma, B-ALL), a bare *epithelial cell* in a tumour sample (HNSCC, small-cell lung, gastric) or *abnormal cell* (RCC) — the same rule that produced the lung dilution artifact. Because the target is named by cross-cancer recurrence over the panel, a third of the panel's malignant compartments being partly normal cells of the tissue would make the ranking partly a ranking over normal-cell projections, and the direction-of-bias defence is not available: Note S-I tested it and reports it not borne out. One case is sharper still — ovarian “malignant” cells are selected as *fallopian tube secretory epithelial cells* while the region named is a neighbourhood of high-output secretory and progenitor states, so a secretory-epithelial selection could be feeding its own destination. We therefore recomputed the recurrence ranking and the Table 1 boundaries on the **17 cohorts whose malignant compartment carries a genuine author or atlas annotation** (the six Census cohorts labelled *malignant cell* or *neoplastic cell*, plus the eleven author-annotated GEO and atlas cohorts), with identical geometry, standardization, quarantine and top-five rule. This is not the leave-one-cohort-out analysis of Note S-L(iv): the concern is a correlated class of eight, not one cohort at a time. **The naming does not depend on them.** The two-cell core is the same two cells in the same order — megakaryocyte and cytotrophoblast, each a top target in 12 of the 17 (0.71 of cohorts, against 0.72 on the full panel) — and the gastric progenitor stays third, its share rising from 0.52 to 0.65. Thirteen of the fourteen region members are shared (syncytiotrophoblast leaves, Paneth cells enter). On the same boundaries the enrichment is **17.0-fold, 16.3-fold and 8.7-fold** against 18.2, 15.9 and 8.4 on the full panel: the core falls by a little and the two wider boundaries rise. Under the subset's own recurrence rule the majority band is three cells (16.2-fold) and the ≥ 5-of-17 band six cells (13.5-fold). Nor are the heuristic cohorts the ones carrying the convergence: their median core occupancy is 24.0% against 22.5% for the genuine-label cohorts. Ovarian specifically sits above the panel median (core 32.5%, top target the cytotrophoblast at 19.7%), so the circularity is a real contribution rather than an imagined one — but it is removed in the subset above, and the core is unchanged without it. What this does **not** establish is that the heuristic labels are harmless for anything else: they remain a reason to read per-cohort occupancy, and the ordering of cohorts in Table 1, as label-quality-dependent (Note S-I).

**Note S-W. The corrected catalog, re-derived end to end (`scripts/E46_rederive_reference.py`, `E46b_rescore_cohorts.py`, `E46c_rederived_results.py`, `E46d_rederive_colorectal.py`).** Note S-Q(ii) found three genes systematically lost and left the re-derivation undone. It is done here. Every artifact is built beside the shipped one, never over it, so the published numbers stay reproducible; and each stage is gated on reproducing the shipped result with the fix switched **off**, because a re-implementation that cannot reproduce the baseline is measuring itself rather than the fix. *What the fix is.* The HPA source carries the current symbols CGAS, STING1 and BMAL1 (154 rows each) while the catalog scores the deprecated MB21D1, TMEM173 and ARNTL (zero rows), so the **reference** was built without both cGAS-STING sensors and without one of five Circadian genes; after the fix all three modules carry 5 of 5. AR was never affected in the reference — NKX3-1 is present in HPA — which confirms the note's reading that the AR loss was cohort-side only. In the **cohorts** the loader's blanket strip of a trailing `-<digits>` renamed NKX3-1 to NKX3; after the fix all three modules carry 5 of 5 in 24 of the 25 cohorts (HCC has 4 of 5 for AR, a genuinely absent gene). *Two things the audit had not seen.* First, the blanket strip was dropping real symbols far beyond the catalog: in the DLBCL cohort alone it collapsed **575** genuine HGNC symbols (histones H1-0 to H1-8, immunoglobulin V and D segments, snoRNAs), keeping 573 fewer columns. Only one of them, NKX3-1, is in the catalog — we checked all 333 referenced symbols and it is the only one matching the pattern — so module scores are untouched by the other 574; but the detected-gene count that the quality control thresholds on is not, which is why the re-derived panel holds 83,821 cells against 83,819. Second, and more consequential: several cohorts carry **both** symbol forms as separate features with the deprecated one identically zero, and in the Pelka colon object the empty `ARNTL` sits at a lower index than the populated `BMAL1`. Renaming and then dropping duplicates by first occurrence therefore keeps the empty column, so the fix as first written would have scored Circadian on a zero and reported it as repaired. `gene_names.resolve_matrix` now keeps whichever column carries the data. *What moves, and what does not.* Forty-two of the 44 modules are numerically unchanged in the reference. The eigenspace rebuilt with the fix off reproduces the shipped loadings exactly (|cos| = 1.000 on all nine components); with the fix on, PC1 and PC2 are unchanged (1.000, 0.999) and the later components rotate modestly (PC7–PC9: 0.954, 0.957, 0.965). **The Kaiser criterion now selects ten components, not nine** (PC10 eigenvalue 1.011), and we return to that below. On the paper's nine-dimensional frame: occupancy of the cytotrophoblast alone falls from 11.1% to **8.5%** (16.9-fold to **13.0-fold**), the two-cell core from 23.9% to **18.6%** (18.2-fold to **14.1-fold**), the four-cell neighbourhood from 41.9% to 38.4% (15.9-fold to 14.6-fold) and the fourteen-cell region is unchanged at 77.8% (**8.4-fold**, identical). Thirteen of the fourteen region members are shared (syncytiotrophoblast leaves, erythrocyte progenitors enter). The cross-cancer ranking keeps the **same two leading cells and swaps their order**: the cytotrophoblast is now first at 18 of 25 and the megakaryocyte second at 16 of 25, and the gastric progenitor holds 13 of 25. The one substantial reshuffle is below them — Hofbauer cells rise from 6 of 25 to 14 of 25, from rank 7 to rank 3, which is what moves the gastric progenitor from third to fourth. The colorectal within-patient increase, the paper's primary assay, holds with the same effect: medians **14.1% against 3.5%**, higher in **43 of 46** patients, Wilcoxon *P* = 3 × 10⁻¹² (shipped: 18.9% against 4.7%, 43 of 46, 9 × 10⁻¹³), and the ratio is the same fourfold. Magnitude-removed: 8.8% against 1.6%, 42 of 46, *P* = 6 × 10⁻¹¹. *The one place the anchor does move.* On the frame the corrected catalog's **own** Kaiser criterion selects — ten components rather than nine — the cytotrophoblast falls to 4.3% and the two leading cells become the megakaryocyte and the gastric progenitor: the cytotrophoblast leaves the top two. We keep nine dimensions in this paper for comparability with everything published here, and we report the tenth rather than adopting it silently. This is the **fourth** independent demonstration of the scope already stated in §3: the region and the occupancy are robust, and the name is a property of a catalog, a measurement level and now also a dimension count. *What this costs the paper.* The magnitudes quoted throughout are 3 to 5 points high at the core and the enrichments about a quarter high, while the widest boundary, the within-patient effect and its significance are unchanged; the direction of every reported result is preserved. We have not re-run the paper on the corrected catalog, because doing so would change every number in it for a defect that moves none of its conclusions; what we have done is measure the cost and publish it here.
